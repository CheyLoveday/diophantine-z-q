# The fine moduli scheme at level three is Shimura's canonical model

For every curve $M$ with a uniformization $\psi$ as in the previous statement, and every scheme $Y$
over $\mathbb Q$ that represents, in the sense of the fine-moduli clauses, abelian surfaces with an
action of $\mathcal O$ and a full level-$3$ structure, there is an isomorphism $f:Y\to M$ over
$\mathbb Q$ carrying the moduli point of $[z,g]$ to $\psi(z,g)$.

It is not printed in this form but follows from published theorems; reaching it from the print takes
further theorems, which are not proved here. No legally readable refereed print read for this
project states it. It combines the identification of the fine moduli scheme, with its complex moduli
description, with Shimura's canonical model (Shimura 1967, Main Theorem I; Deligne's moduli
construction in Sém. Bourbaki 389) and the uniqueness of canonical models (Cornut–Vatsal §3.1.2;
Deligne 5.5), applied at level $K_3$ alone. Boutot and Carayol state both structures on $S_U$ but do
not compare them. It stays a glue step until one of these sources is read. It is a hypothesis, not
proved here.
