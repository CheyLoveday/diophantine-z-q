# Shimura's canonical model at level three and the reciprocity law at CM points

With $K_3=\{k\in\hat{\mathcal O}^\times: k\equiv1\bmod 3\}$, there are a smooth curve $M$ over
$\mathbb Q$ and a map $\psi$ from $(\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R)\times(B\otimes\mathbb A_f)^\times$
onto $M(\mathbb C)$, holomorphic in the first variable, whose fibres are the classes of
$B^\times\backslash\big((\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R)\times(B\otimes\mathbb A_f)^\times/K_3\big)$,
such that, for one sign $\varepsilon$, for every imaginary quadratic field $K$ with an embedding
$q:K\to B$, every CM point $z$ of $q$ in the upper half-plane and every idele $\lambda$ of $K$, the
automorphism $\sigma=\mathrm{rec}_K(\lambda)$ satisfies
$\sigma\,\psi(z,g)=\psi(z,\hat q(\lambda)^{\varepsilon}g)$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is Cornut and Vatsal's description of Shimura's canonical model (§3.1.1) at level $K_3$, with
their Lemma 3.11, which they derive on p. 299 from the reciprocity law for CM points. Their
reciprocity map sends uniformizers to geometric Frobenius and Galois acts on points on the left
(§3.2.4); with the arithmetic Artin map used here, $\sigma=\mathrm{rec}_K(\lambda)$ is the Artin
symbol of $\lambda^{-1}$. The holomorphic identification of their symmetric space with
$\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R$ is their Remark 3.15, and the level-$K_3$ forms are their §3.2 and
§3.2.5. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
