Here `End(A)` denotes the full geometric endomorphism ring. It is the maximal order in the level-one theorem. This distinction matters at $p\mid N$.

## Smallest remaining comparison

Fix the actual supersingular reference $(A_0,C_0)$ over the geometric residue field at the detector. For each supersingular enhanced pair $(A,C)$, the missing geometric comparison is the **completed Hom-lattice description**, with the rational coordinates fixed by a quasi-isogeny from the reference. It should identify
$$
I(A,C)=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}(A_0,A): f(C_0)\subseteq C\}
$$
with a locally principal right ideal of $\operatorname{End}(A_0,C_0)$. Its local left order should identify with $\operatorname{End}(A,C)_p$ by composition. Away from $q$, the completed Hom lattice must be the integral Tate maps preserving the two lattices of the level chains; at $q$, the completed geometric endomorphism order must be identified with the division order, with the chosen tangent scalar map transported to it. This identifies the actual local labels and the individual labelled quotients. A coarse Hecke divisor only counts unlabelled quotient targets, so it cannot provide those readings.

The new factorization and saturation results mean the comparison need not assume a separate theorem saying that a map killing the geometric kernel factors through a prime-to-$q$ quotient. That step is now proved. Away from $q$, the outstanding work is to use the full rank-four geometric Hom lattice and Tate rank two to turn saturation into the completed lattice identification, and to choose the integral basis putting the geometric chain in the split model. The preservation condition itself is proved on actual integral maps. At $q$, the two residue choices and their distinction are formal once the geometric order and its scalar action are transported. The same completed coordinates must give the actual reference class of the existing detector's geometric comparison. This is about supersingular curves over one field; it does not assert anything about the image or injectivity of CM reduction.

In the covariant convention $I=\operatorname{Hom}(A_0,A)$ transported into $\operatorname{End}^0(A_0)$, pull the target Tate lattice of a quotient back by the quotient isogeny. For a local generator $a_p$ of the ideal acting on the source, the pulled-back target lattice is $a_p^{-1}T_p(A)$. The same factor multiplies $g_{0,p}$ on the left. Thus an idele of the positive ideal class gives $a^{-1}z_0$ in these right-ideal coordinates. The source annihilator is a different, left ideal, computed by the reverse Hom group. The positive reading $a z_0$ in a weighted sum can be obtained by reindexing the inverse convention with the quadratic character. The pointwise assertion uses the actual convention. No label is conjugated to accomplish this.

Once the geometric reads are supplied, pass `orientedCorrespondence_of_inverseReferenceReads` to the existing `Consumer.point_reduction_of_orientedCorrespondence`. Its coarse class equality is the forgetful image of the inverse class coordinates and its parametrization hypothesis stays separate. The resulting pointwise equality feeds the sum in tex 9279–9295; if that sum is indexed by positive ideal classes, `sum_mul_inv_eq_of_invariant` supplies the change of variables.
