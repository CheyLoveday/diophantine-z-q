In Voight's convention $E[I]$ is the intersection of the scheme kernels of the elements of the left $O$-ideal $I$, $E_I=E/E[I]$, and the ideal functor is contravariant. No right-ideal restatement is assumed. The algebraic conversion would send $I$ to $\overline I$ and $O_R(I)$ to $O_L(\overline I)$ under standard quaternion conjugation. The scoped local result needs neither a left-ideal print nor its mirror.

The Lean hypothesis is `A.dim = 1` and `∀ P : A.Pt k, P ^ q = 1 → P = 1`, with `k` algebraically closed of characteristic `q`. This is the geometric $q$-rank-zero condition $E[q](\bar k)=\{0\}$ on the elliptic curve, with $1$ the identity in Lean's multiplicative notation. It tests the geometric points of the multiplication kernel. That kernel as a group scheme can be nonreduced and have degree $q^2$. The comparison with the standard geometric definition gives no mathematical difference over an algebraically closed field.
