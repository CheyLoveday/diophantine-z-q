## The reference

Let $A$ be the reduction of the chosen reference curve over the residue field of the detector place, with its specialized $\mathcal O_F$-action and its cyclic level subgroup. The forward inert criterion applies to its reduced Weierstrass equation once its point group and order action have been identified with those of $A$. The existing Deuring candidate gives some presentation of the rational endomorphism algebra. The detector presentation $(-1,-q)$ has the same norm-form ramification set by the tree's explicit calculation, using $q \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$. `VoightMainThm1461` states existence at the specified set. Its uniqueness clause is missing from the Lean type. `QuaternionRamificationUniqueness` states just this clause restricted to the two places used here. This input compares rational quaternion presentations solely by ramification and has no geometric or class-set data.

Transport of the Deuring order preserves the full lattice and maximality. At $q$ the actual tensor completion of $\operatorname{End}(A)$ is the explicit division valuation order. The comparison sends each $1 \otimes f$ to the image of the same endomorphism $f$ under the pinned frame. Since $N$ is a unit at $q$, the completed enhanced order there is unchanged. At the other primes the Tate module puts the $p$-primary cyclic level in the standard integral chain. The matrix profile includes exponent zero and $p=2$. The split calculation takes the geometric Tate rank as input, available as `HDec.CMSerreTate.P1.originalTateModuleFree_proved`. The global reference representative patches the local order changes and can use the identity wherever the two completions agree.

The cotangent space in the proof is the maximal ideal modulo its square, at the identity of the actual scheme. The field-linear pullback agrees with `AbVarBC.MulRigidity.cotangentMap` on each element. Maps from square-zero test rings detect the cotangent classes of the product, so the projections span the product cotangent space. The two unit sections then give the sum law for the group multiplication. Together with composition, this makes the scalar in a trivialization of the one-dimensional cotangent space a ring homomorphism. Every vector has that scalar, including on the dual tangent. On the good model the specialization test uses one generator of the integral differential module with nonzero reduction. The generic scalar of the action on that generator is the chosen CM embedding. Its reduction is the scalar of the actual specialized endomorphism. The scalar on the geometric endomorphism ring extends to the division completion and factors through its residue field by a unique embedding. Applied to the fixed chart `DivisionJoin.ambientIntegralEquiv q`, this reads the detector residue character on the specialized $\mathcal O_F$-action. Choosing a different detector place or CM type can select the other residue embedding.

The tree has no common integral modular-curve morphism realizing these two evaluations.

`ParametrizationFibresAt` asks for a morphism over the detector base, its generic values on the given CM sections, and its special values on modular points. This comparison is a fibre calculation on schemes, independent of the arithmetic point reduction `red`, the completed Hom dictionary, and the class attachment. In `parametrization_commutes_of_fibres`, `hred` identifies the restrictions of an elliptic-curve section with the coordinate reduction already used by the consumer.
