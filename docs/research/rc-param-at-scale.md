# Rational modular parametrization at the fixed scale

**Scoped stop.** For the actual elliptic curve `Ecurve ℓ` at its given analytic scale, the missing input is that the two analytic coordinate germs at infinity belong to the **specified rational function field** `modularFunctionField (newformLevel (Ecurve ℓ))`. The existing modularity, period and curve model interfaces do not supply that assertion. We prove that the rational generic map extends to a dominant morphism over ℚ, and prove the comparison of its rational sections with the analytic germs. We also prove the analytic cusp comparison with one nonzero rational scalar at all the cusps, and give the two field-valued consumer theorems conditional on the existing `AlgebraicParametrizationAtScale`. The main proposition is not discharged. No print or axiom was added.

The existing Milne source audit records a citation gap at tex 1781–1788: the cited Theorem 11.19 and Remark 11.18 cover the map and quotient used there, but a normalized newform is supplied separately by Proposition 11.21, and the cited pair does not state the Hecke equivariance in 1788. That audit also reports two unused misprints in Conjecture 11.17, listed below. No new mathematical error in the manuscript was found.

## The exact stop

Let `E = Ecurve ℓ`, `N = newformLevel E`, `F(τ) = eichlerPotential f τ`, and keep the proposed `κ` and `Lat` fixed. The analytic affine coordinates are
`xₐ(τ) = ℘ₗₐₜ(κ F(τ)) - b₂(E)/12` and
`yₐ(τ) = (℘′ₗₐₜ(κ F(τ)) - a₁(E)xₐ(τ) - a₃(E))/2`.
The first missing proposition, written with the same coefficients used by `X.sectionExpansion`, is:

```lean
∃ (x y : modularFunctionField N) (T : ℝ), ∀ τ : UpperHalfPlane, T < τ.im →
  HasSum (fun n : ℤ => (((x : LaurentSeries ℚ).coeff n : ℚ) : ℂ) *
    Complex.exp (2 * Real.pi * Complex.I * (n : ℂ) * (τ : ℂ))) (xCoordinate E f Lat κ τ) ∧
  HasSum (fun n : ℤ => (((y : LaurentSeries ℚ).coeff n : ℚ) : ℂ) *
    Complex.exp (2 * Real.pi * Complex.I * (n : ℂ) * (τ : ℂ))) (yCoordinate E f Lat κ τ)
```

This says only that the two germs are realized by elements of the embedded subfield ℚ(j(q), j(q^N)). It requires neither a morphism nor any comparison at a chart point away from the tail. The checked affine comparison accepts a field embedding with these two expansions on its generic coordinates. To get that embedding from the displayed proposition, transport the affine equation by uniqueness of Laurent coefficients and use the pole of the x-coordinate to prove that it is transcendental over ℚ. The existing `NewformS13.modularPointFieldHom` is a precedent in the other rational q-field presentation. Finally, comparison of regular germs has to be turned into equality of projective values, including the point at infinity: compare the residues of `X/Z` and `Y/Z` in the affine chart and use `Z/Y` in the chart at the identity to account for the poles.

The source model already identifies its function field with the chosen rational Laurent subfield. Its `sectionExpansion` runs from rational sections to analytic representatives; it does not assert the converse. The existing `ModularCurve.modularFunctionField_eq_full` compares subfields generated by degeneracies of j, with no classification of meromorphic modular functions. The `NewformPeriodsG1` route starts with rational coordinates and recovers periods; its local inverse need not have its constant term based at infinity. Neither of these gives the missing proposition.

There are two possible routes to the missing proposition. The analytic route still needs rational Laurent coefficients for the **coordinates** (the primitive coefficients are now checked), meromorphic extension in every cusp coordinate, and a converse rational q-expansion theorem identifying the resulting functions with the specified field. The geometric route requires the actual Jacobian realization and the following comparison for the chosen quotient and isogeny: the chart pullback of the model differential \(dx/(2y+a_1x+a_3)\) along the based map \(\alpha q_f\circ\mathrm{AJ}_{\infty}\) is \(\kappa\,dF=2\pi i\kappa f(\tau)\,d\tau\). The basepoint then removes the integration constant. A geometric existence statement for an unspecified morphism or an unspecified scalar would leave that comparison open.
