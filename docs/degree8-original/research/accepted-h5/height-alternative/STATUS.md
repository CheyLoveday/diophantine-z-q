Its height coefficient has logarithm less than 1,391,000. The original coefficient has logarithm greater than 1,398,435.84. Isogeny transfer over a field of degree less than 2^35 fits within the remaining margin. This uses only an elementary count of reduced residue classes modulo 210, not a computed prime table or a new analytic estimate.

At n=4608 it has logarithm greater than `663552*9 = 5971968`, already far above the original budget, whose logarithm is less than `165888*9 = 1492992`. Thus that coarse route does not certify the enlarged descent, while the sharper published bound does.

The second change avoids demanding that a splitting cochain factor through the original finite Galois group. Guitart's published construction controls the rational factor by the coefficient field generated by the cochain values. The fragment proves a sufficient condition: coefficient-field degree at most 4608, splitting the quaternion algebra, and trivial inertia outside the existing bad support. It also derives the bound on the field needed for isogeny transfer. These are conditions to prove, not additional permitted theorem assumptions.

This meets the broader GL2-type definition in the published height theorem. The coefficient-field and inertia bounds are still unresolved.

## Remaining obstruction

Neither the order-two property expected for the QM cocycle nor Tate's vanishing theorem alone bounds the coefficient field or the ramification of a splitting map. The missing proof must use this family's actual cocycle, or establish a quantitative splitting theorem with the stated numerical costs. The field defining all compatible isogenies only makes the cocycle finite; it does not make it a scalar coboundary on that finite Galois group.

One possible direction is to prove that the sign component is supported at a fixed small set of primes and that the positive component has bounded squareclass rank. This is not proved here. In particular, no claim is made that the sign support equals the ramification support of the quaternion algebra merely because that algebra has discriminant 210.

The descent worker has identified a more precise reason for that caution. The compatible isogenies act on the two-dimensional tangent space over L, so their matrix cocycle identifies the natural-action Brauer image of the full scalar cocycle with the quaternion class. After decomposing the scalar cocycle into positive and sign components, the sign class also contains the contribution of the positive component. Degree squareclasses supported on divisors of 210 can pair with varying quadratic characters. Their Hilbert-symbol contributions need not have fixed ramification support. This observation does not construct a counterexample within the family, but it prevents concluding a bound on the sign component from the discriminant 210 alone.

No unconditional alternative bound with the original coefficient and exponent 24 has been established. No impossibility of such a bound is asserted. The result of this task is a larger numerical target and an explicit coefficient-field route for the descent worker to test.
