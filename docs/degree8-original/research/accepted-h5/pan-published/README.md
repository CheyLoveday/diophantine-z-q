# Published formulation of Pan's regular modularity result

The published-source requirement can be met by the inspected mathematical statements in Calegari's ICM chapter and Newton's journal article. Both explicitly report Pan's full result for primes at least five. Their statements agree with the inspected author preprint, Theorem 1.0.4 and its referenced Conjecture 1.0.1. This packet does not claim a comparison with the inaccessible AMS version of record.

For the application, the result has the following scope. A continuous irreducible representation of the absolute Galois group of the rationals into GL(2) over an algebraic closure of the p-adic numbers is modular up to twist if it is odd, unramified outside finitely many primes, and de Rham at p with two distinct Hodge--Tate weights, where p is at least five. Potential semistability can be used in place of the de Rham condition. There is no residual-image hypothesis in this range. Neither published exposition supplies a fresh proof of Pan's theorem; both give an explicit mathematical statement, not merely a bibliographic citation.

## Inspected sources

* Frank Calegari, *Reciprocity in the Langlands program since Fermat's Last Theorem*, ICM 2022, volume 2, pages 610--651, EMS Press, **2023**, DOI 10.4171/ICM2022/101. Printed page 624, final paragraph of section 7, states the full modularity-up-to-twist result. Section 1.1, pages 611--612, gives the geometric representation context. PDF pages are 15 and 2--3. Reference 137 on printed page 649 cites Pan's 2019 arXiv paper. The chapter carries copyright 2022; the publisher's volume page dates publication to 15 December 2023. These dates should not be conflated.
* James Newton, *Modularity of Galois Representations and Langlands Functoriality*, Journal of the Indian Institute of Science 102 (2022), 861--884, DOI 10.1007/s41745-022-00305-0. Section 3.3, printed page 870, states the same full regular result and the corresponding weight-at-least-two interpretation. Section 2.4, printed page 863, defines geometric as finite ramification and de Rham at p. Continuity is fixed in section 2.1; irreducibility is part of the Fontaine--Mazur--Langlands conjecture formulated as Conjecture 2.5.1. Reference 76 cites the Pan preprint. The publisher identifies this as a review article; it is an authored, peer-reviewed mathematical exposition rather than a separate proof of the cited theorem.
* Lue Pan, *The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in the residually reducible case*, arXiv:1901.07166v2, 3 September 2021. Conjecture 1.0.1, PDF page 2, specifies continuity, irreducibility, finite ramification, potential semistability, and oddness. Theorem 1.0.4, PDF page 5, adds distinct Hodge--Tate weights and only an extra restriction at p=3. For p>=5 that restriction is vacuous. PDF page 7 fixes the cyclotomic character's Hodge--Tate weight as -1 and geometric Frobenius for local class field theory. The associated journal publication is JAMS 35 (2022), 1031--1169, DOI 10.1090/jams/991. We inspected the preprint, not the journal text.

The application's Tate-module weights {0,-1} are distinct in Pan's convention. The further conclusion that the modular weight is two and that the twist is finite uses the application's own weight and determinant argument. The sources here do not assert a conductor bound or a bound on the coefficient field, and none is imported from them.

## Retrieval limits and rejected substitutes

The AMS DOI, direct journal PDF paths, and public alternate AMS paths returned HTTP 403; logs are retained. Crossref identifies a version-of-record URL but does not expose its theorem text. OpenAlex supplies only the submitted arXiv version. The author's publication page links the preprint. Thus no AMS PDF hash or exact version-of-record textual equivalence is asserted.

The published Dieulefait--Pacetti paper, DOI 10.1007/s13398-023-01478-8, Theorems 1.4 and 1.6, was also inspected. Theorem 1.6 explicitly gives the normalized conclusion for residual reducibility, but Theorem 1.4 retains a residual restriction after quadratic base change. That pair alone was not substituted for the unrestricted result. Pan's Forum of Mathematics Pi paper concerns additional irregular cases and was not used to replace the regular theorem.

## Recommended citation use

Retain Pan as the theorem's author and journal citation. Cite the inspected author version as such, and cite Calegari, pages 611--612 and 624, for the accessible published formulation. Newton, sections 2.4--2.5 and 3.3, gives an independent published corroboration. Suggested bibliography keys are `calegari-icm2022` and `newton-modularity2022`. Do not write that the AMS text was inspected or that these expositions establish byte identity with it.

The PDF files remain research evidence in this work directory. Public packaging can retain the URLs, hashes, and this application ledger without copying the PDFs.
