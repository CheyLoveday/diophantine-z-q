# Independent scalar-cocycle obstruction review

The narrow obstruction passes. I checked the published [Voight chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_43), equations 43.1.2–43.1.3 and the moduli theorem. The scalar map j(b,z) maps the source lattice into O b v_z, commutes with the whole left O action, and has degree Nrd(b)^2. Rational scaling changes its positive square-root degree by a square. Since the compatible Hom space at a non-CM point is one-dimensional over Q, this computes the intrinsic Galois degree character from its W label. The unmarked degree-one map would not compute that character. The proof uses the correct map.

The tangent representation gives a semilinear descent datum on M_2(L). Its invariant algebra contains the quaternion algebra and has the same dimension four, so it equals that algebra. The natural-action Brauer image of the scalar cocycle is therefore [B]. The positive cocycle is the trivial-action coboundary of the positive square root of d. Comparing trivial and natural coboundaries gives the stated cup products, hence the sign class [B] plus their sum. At a valuation-one contact with zero, the inertia degree class is 105. Since the fixed degree multipliers are p-units and B splits there, the odd-prime Hilbert-symbol formula gives exactly the character (105/p).

For any continuous scalar splitting beta in the discrete algebraic coefficient group, epsilon=beta^2/d is a finite character with values in the coefficient field. Its square-root boundary is the sign class. Under local reciprocity, the wild unit factor at odd p has odd-order finite characters and the valuation factor admits square roots. On the cyclic group of order p-1, the squaring map fails precisely for characters whose order has full 2-part. A nonzero boundary thus forces an inertia value of order divisible by 2^k, where k=v_2(p-1). Its 2-primary part lies in E and is primitive, giving [E:Q] at least 2^(k-1).

If beta factors through an extension of the original finite fiber group, so does epsilon because d already factors through that fiber group. Consequently 2^k divides the extension order. The original group order divides 576, whose 2-part is 2^6, so the kernel has 2-part at least 2^(k-6). This argument does not need the extension to be central.

The CRT progression is reduced and has the required Legendre symbol; Dirichlet supplies primes with arbitrarily large k outside every fixed bad set. I checked [González–Rotger Theorem 5.8(1)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath/58/4/58_4_927/_pdf): the ring class field is the compositum of the CM field and the point field. Degree at most sixteen over the rational quotient therefore bounds the order class number by sixteen. Classical class-number growth and the order class-number formula give only finitely many rational CM parameters. They can be removed from each infinite valuation-one progression.

Thus the uniform coefficient-degree bounds 2304 and 4608 fail for this scalar-splitting strategy; k=14 already forces degree at least 8192. A uniformly bounded-order extension resolving the scalar cocycle fails as well. This conclusion does not refute H5, does not rule out every auxiliary construction, and does not object to the baseline's radical-dependent coefficient fields. A positive level-height route must be reviewed independently.
