## B1. The primary hypotheses at p=2

### The field and support

Nekovar 2.1, printed p. 51, sets the standing coefficient-ring convention for Chapters 2-11: a complete Noetherian local ring R. Chapter 5, printed p. 113, additionally assumes a finite residue field of characteristic p. Section 5.1 takes a global field K of characteristic different from p and a finite support S containing the p-adic and archimedean places. Its condition (P) says that K has no real prime when p=2. The opening of Chapter 6, printed p. 135, expressly retains those assumptions, including (P).

Here p=2, K is imaginary quadratic, and S contains every place above 2N together with the actual additional ramification. Thus (P) holds. No real-place modification from Section 5.7 is needed in this ring-class layer. This conclusion is not transferable to the rational Kato layer without its separate treatment of real places. S03A:3-16 identifies the field and finite coefficient systems; RL:12-20 and RL:380-403 identify the support and the actual Selmer cone.

Nekovar 5.1.4 on printed p. 114 is local Tate duality for finite discrete p-primary coefficients. It does not impose odd p, ordinary reduction of an elliptic curve, or irreducibility of its residual representation. The later 5.2.4 and 5.4.3 also impose no such extra condition.

### The actual finite rings and the choice of dualizing object

At each retained stage use `R=A_i` or `R=A_i[G]`, where `A_i=(Z/2^i)[t]/(t^i)` and G is a finite elementary abelian 2-group. These are finite commutative local rings. For the group ring, its maximal ideal is generated by 2, t, and the elements g-1; its residue field is F_2. It is Noetherian and complete. The coefficient representation is finite free of rank two over R and has a continuous action through a finite quotient, hence is an admissible module and is a bounded complex in degree zero.

The following gives a direct check that the Matlis choice in the primary applies with an R-valued pairing. Let tau extract the coefficient of `t^(i-1)` at the identity group element. The pairing `tau(ab)` identifies R with `Hom_(Z/2^i)(R,Z/2^i)`: in the basis `t^j g`, the dual basis is `t^(i-1-j) g^(-1)`. Embedding Z/2^i into Q_2/Z_2 identifies this latter module with `Hom_Z2(R,Q_2/Z_2)`. By adjunction it is injective as an R-module. Its socle is the dual of R's residue field and has dimension one. Since R has finite length, that socle is essential. Thus R is an injective hull I of F_2, as in Nekovar 2.3.1-2.3.2, printed p. 52.

Consequently we may take the bounded injective complex `J=R=I` in degree zero. The module and its dual have finite-type cohomology. This satisfies case (i) of 5.2.4 and 5.4.3, and case (A) of 6.2.5, with shift n=0. There is no appeal to semisimplicity of F_2[G] and no division by |G|.

The top-coefficient tau only verifies the injective/Frobenius property at an individual stage. It is not used as the arithmetic invariant map under changes of i. Nekovar 5.2.1, printed pp. 114-115, constructs the coefficient-valued local invariant map; 5.4.1, printed pp. 125-126, constructs the compatible compact global trace with shift -3. The retained cup product takes values in R(1). This is the distinction explicitly preserved at S03A:146-148 and LD:26-28.

### Local and global duality maps

On printed p. 116, Proposition 5.2.4 says that the local cup-product duality maps are isomorphisms for the Matlis finite/cofinite cases or the dualizing-complex finite-type case. On printed p. 127, Proposition 5.4.3 gives the corresponding compact/global duality maps; its proof reduces to finite discrete p-primary modules and Poitou-Tate duality. These propositions apply to the finite R and finite representation just checked.

The Weil pairing, together with conjugate transport, supplies the perfect representation pairing needed in 6.2.6. Conjugation inverts Psi_i; it fixes each rational quadratic character and hence the universal quadratic character, which is its own inverse. Therefore the two coefficient actions cancel in the pairing into R(1). The rank-two alternating Weil form is perfect even though the residual representation has full rational two-torsion. Alternation is not being used as a substitute for isotropy of a chosen local condition.

Nekovar 6.2.1.3 on printed p. 138 requires an actual null-homotopy of the restricted local product. Section 6.2.7 on p. 140 strengthens orthogonality to the quotient-to-dual quasi-isomorphism. Theorem 6.3.4 on p. 143 then has a local error term, and gives the global isomorphism precisely when those errors vanish. It does not independently prove Kummer self-orthogonality and does not say that every relaxed complex is self-dual.

The local objects in this application meet that exact contract as follows.

* At 2N use the actual twisted Kummer map `B(K_v)^wedge_2[-1] -> RΓ(K_v,T_2B)`, with finite torsion retained. RL:285-346 and S03A:114-135 construct it before coefficient extension. A two-term free resolution gives both the degree-zero torsion and the degree-one Kummer image after reduction. The finite local Kummer-annihilator theorem is the separate classical local input from Milne cited there. Given that input, the restricted integral cup map vanishes because all its values vanish modulo every power of 2. The possible ambiguity `Hom_D(M tensor^L M,Z_2[-1])` is zero. The quotient-to-dual map is a quasi-isomorphism modulo 2 by the Kummer annihilator in degree one and local H^2 paired with B[2] in degree two. Its perfect cone is zero by derived Nakayama. Thus the exact primary null-homotopy and quotient conditions are present, not inferred from equal dimensions. This also applies at dyadic additive or supersingular places; no ordinary filtration has been inserted.
* At a full local condition the orthogonal side is strict zero. The product with zero is literally zero; these are complementary conditions under the local duality just checked. At active and moving primes they must not be identified integrally with each other. The source uses that identification only after the specified local contraction.
* At a derivative prime, RL:358-378 and RD:64-161 use the actual split local model with D^0, finite plane F, singular plane S, and D^2. Here the word strict means the truncated D^0 condition, not the full-condition complement zero used at the other primes. The retained pure-plane products vanish, with the extra precision needed for the characteristic-two singular product. Their zero homotopies agree on D^0. Thus these are compatible local conditions in the sense of 6.2, not a deduction from skew-symmetry at 2.

This checks the Nekovar interface. The separate classical finite Kummer-annihilator theorem and the finite arithmetic construction of derivative classes have not been replaced by a claim that Nekovar alone proves them.

All applications of the primary duality theorem can be made at these original finite rings. The later rings `O=completion of Lambda_(2)` and the outer valuation ring do not have the same finite-residue-field setup. The argument there is transport of the retained finite maps and homotopy identities through the constructed limits and base changes, as S03B:15-39 states. This audit does not invoke 5.2.4 or 6.3.4 afresh over an arbitrary outer ring. In particular, no unsupported infinite-prime duality theorem is needed for B1.

### Functoriality is a condition on the maps and their homotopies

On printed pp. 144-145, Section 6.4.1 lists representation maps, maps of local-condition complexes, homotopies making their local squares commute, compatibility of the representation pairings, and a second-order homotopy relating the local isotropy homotopies. Proposition 6.4.2 gives compatibility of Selmer cup products and 6.4.3 gives compatibility of the adjoint duality maps. Section 6.4.4 permits all those homotopies to be zero when the displayed products and squares are literally compatible.

It would be insufficient to say only that two complexes are quasi-isomorphic. In the unused-prime comparison below the maps are the original inclusions of local conditions and identity maps on the old data. All old homotopies are kept. At the new place the strict factor is zero, so the pulled-back homotopies agree as zero. This explicitly supplies the second-order compatibility. It does not assert that the old Kummer isotropy homotopies are themselves zero.

## B2. The universal character is trivial at every place above 2N

The current S03A:120-122 mentions only that Psi_i is trivial. This is not, by itself, enough to identify the universal coefficient representation with a scalar extension of the fixed local twist. Opus is correct to request the missing epsilon statement.

The actual common graph gives it. S03:466-468 has h_0=1 and every fixed local type unchanged. More explicitly, T3C:108-112 forms a packet with even multiplicity of each full common label and places a packet at each nonzero activation form. Every added primary vertex has neutral full label. At every address the total active label is therefore zero, including the local squareclass coordinates at all rational p dividing 2N. S09:249-260 and S09:320-330 use that same graph at the single final theta stage. Consequently h_x lies in `Q_p^(times 2)` for every x and every p|2N. Because K is split there, the same statement holds in every K_v above p. The base x=0 has h_0=1 and also satisfies it.

For any `g in G_(K_v)` and any actual binary sign character x of G,

`x(epsilon(g)) = chi_(h_x)(g) = 1`.

All sign characters separate the elements of the elementary abelian group G, so `epsilon(g)=1`. This is an identity of the actual group-valued Galois character before passing to A_i[G]. It must not be deduced merely from reductions of the sign characters modulo 2, where the two signs coincide.

Psi_i is also trivial at 2N by its actual construction, S03A:12 and RL:477. Thus the universal coefficient action at each such place is genuinely the scalar extension of the fixed local action, with the inherited Kummer map and inherited null-homotopy. For the varying scalar R-argument, a nontrivial fixed local quadratic type is instead carried by the actual twist B=E^(h); it is never dropped.

## B3. The second e_q belongs to the functional

The point norm at an unused split q is the single scalar

`e_q = a_q - z - z^(-1)`.

It already contains both places/orientations above q, as the actual unaveraged norm (4.1), S03A:63-73, and RD:1281-1287 show. The sentence at S03A:270, 'Its conjugate supplies a second copy', does not explain the second occurrence in the class-functional determinant coordinate. Here is the required comparison with the actual local maps.

Work first with one fixed finite stage and one character x for which q is unused. By B1, support change identifies the primitive smaller-support object with one having the unramified condition `U_q -> L_q=RΓ(K_q,M)` at the two q-places. The relaxed object has condition L_q. Its orthogonal object has strict condition 0 there. Write

`i : P -> R` for primitive-to-relaxed on the primal side,

`j : S -> P^perp` for strict-to-primitive on the paired side.

Only the q-condition is indicated by these names. At every other place retain the existing primal and orthogonal conditions, including their old Kummer and derivative homotopies. `P^perp` is not identified with P integrally at old full-condition places. Such identifications are made only on the generic test where the explicit contractions apply. This convention avoids asserting false integral self-duality of the relaxed complex.

The maps i and j are induced by the actual inclusions `U_q -> L_q` and `0 -> U_q`, identity maps on the global representation, and identity maps on the old data. Use the common pair with q-conditions `(0,U_q)`. Its maps to the pairs `(0,L_q)` and `(U_q,U_q)` compare the relaxed pairing and the primitive pairing respectively. At old places the local squares commute and the chosen homotopies are identical. At q, the product with strict 0 vanishes; every pulled-back unramified isotropy homotopy is zero on that factor. Thus the first-order homotopies and the difference of the isotropy homotopies are zero, so a zero second-order homotopy satisfies Nekovar 6.4.1. This is the exact compatibility behind the identity

`beta_R(v, i(w)) = beta_P(j(v), w)`

in the derived category, by 6.4.2-6.4.3. It is not an assertion that arbitrary choices of dual functionals agree.

Now take the same finite weighted Heegner class on the two supports. The unaveraged norm relation gives

`z_R = e_q i(z_P)`

in the actual Selmer cohomology. The equality of global classes lifts to this equality because the local degree-zero maps are onto and the degree-one maps are injective; the source retains its boundary, S03A:105-111 and S03A:224-225. RD:1224-1244 requires comparison through those original target cochains. Hence this class relation survives the same coefficientwise limit with its maps.

On the generic test the q-local complexes are acyclic, so the comparison maps have their canonical inverses in the derived category. The functional is obtained by lifting the conjugate actual class to the orthogonal object and applying the retained pairing, exactly as RD:439-464 and S03A:189-216 specify. Let z_S be that lift for z_R and z_P^perp the corresponding lift for z_P. The same generic comparisons give

`j(z_S) = e_q^c z_P^perp`.

Using the preceding pairing identity gives

`lambda_R(i(w)) = beta_R(z_S,i(w)) = e_q^c beta_P(z_P^perp,w) = e_q^c lambda_P(w)`.

Conjugation interchanges z and z^(-1) and fixes a_q, so `e_q^c=e_q`. This is the second factor. One e_q comes from z_R; one comes from lambda_R. No additional point norm, place count, class-number average, or normalization multiplier is inserted.

The two-place local quotient separately has one rank-two differential `z Fr_q-q` and one `z^(-1) Fr_q-q`, in degrees 1 and 2. Its determinant is

`delta_q = q^4 l_q`, where `l_q=det(1-q^(-1)z Fr_q) det(1-q^(-1)z^(-1)Fr_q)`.

In the inverse-determinant convention a generically acyclic quotient contributes its reciprocal to the class-functional coordinate. The support triangle therefore gives `l_q u_rel=e_q^2 epsilon_q u_prim`. The comparison is first an identity in the generic field. No assertion that l_q or e_q is nonzero at t=0 is needed.

The unit qualification is also preserved. The support triangle is a mapping cone over the original A_i. Its determinant-line map in graded direct-sum bases is a sign. Passing to retained finite free homotopy-equivalent models contributes only units from bounded acyclic free cones. The scalar q^(-4) is odd. Keeping these units and their inverses through the same finite coefficient limits gives `epsilon_q in Lambda^times`, not merely in O^times. This is the original-line mechanism at S03A:276-287 and EPS:11-17,29-31,53-55. A later O-minimal-model basis cannot be substituted without recording its determinant.

B3 disposition: the missing argument is supplied by the actual inclusions, retained boundaries, and primary functoriality. The existing S03A:270 wording should be replaced by this class-versus-functional explanation. The identity (4.7), the universal correction eta, and the corrected coordinate keep their stated factors; there is no extra or missing e_q.
