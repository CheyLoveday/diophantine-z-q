This is an additive source audit. It leaves the raw sources, all delivered drafts, and every earlier receipt unchanged.

The result is a scoped affirmative derivation for the actual non-CM pole family. The two issues called M1 and M2 in the new Opus report do not expose a new mathematical gap in that family. M1 is already expanded in the current manuscript. Sections 3 and 4 below supply the weighted-adjointness and relative-metaplectic arguments needed for M2. ## 2. M1: geometry does not supply the weighted identity by itself

The Opus report's old consumer, called V2 in its packet, compressed the conclusion into a citation to Cornut-Jetchev. That citation alone does not give (1). The raw proof CO315-318 and CO518-531 contains three separate ingredients: the chosen root parity, the embedding stabilizer, and the paired orientation flips. Current08 has them at O.3 and in lines 240-268.

### 2.1 Primary geometric statements and their hypotheses

Ribet, "On modular representations of Gal(Qbar/Q) arising from modular forms", Inventiones 100 (1990), pp.438-443, uses a positive integer M prime to the reduction prime q. There is no squarefree or oddness hypothesis on M. Its extra prime p can be any different prime not dividing M and is irrelevant to Proposition 3.3.

On p.439 an enhanced elliptic curve is a supersingular curve with a cyclic subgroup of order M. Its endomorphism ring is an Eichler order of level M, described as the intersection of the two endpoint maximal orders. Proposition 3.3 and Variant 3.4 on p.440 identify the enhanced supersingular isomorphism classes with the double-coset set and with right ideal classes. Remark 3.5(b), p.441, identifies Frobenius with right multiplication by an idele which is a uniformizer at q and 1 elsewhere. The explicit enhanced Tate-lattice construction keeps the cyclic subgroup. Thus M=N and q=ell_0 meet the exact hypotheses, including arbitrary powers of 2 in N.

The good-prime Brandt correspondence is also read directly from this construction. A cyclic p-isogeny, p prime to N ell_0, replaces the local enhanced Tate lattice by one of its p+1 index-p neighbors. The right double-coset action is this same lattice replacement. A partial Atkin-Lehner operation exchanges the two endpoint lattices at p^e||N; its local normalizer is represented by a matrix such as [0,1;-p^e,0]. This reverses that local cyclic quotient and leaves the other level data fixed. No separate multiplicity assertion is imported into the weighted sum.

Cornut-Jetchev, arXiv:1012.0725v1, gives the oriented CM lifting and its geometric interpretation. The primary pages checked are:
- p.5, hypotheses H.1-H.3 and the equivariant adelic diagram in section 3.2;
- p.9, Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2 and the description of the fixed fiber by optimal embeddings in section 3.6;
- p.15, sections 4.9-4.10 and Corollary 4.1;
- p.16, the explanation of the geometric optimal-embedding map.

Here F=Q, the source algebra is M_2(Q), the changed set is {ell_0}, and the target is the definite algebra ramified at ell_0 and infinity. H.1 holds because the source is split. H.2 is 1+0+1 even. H.3 holds because ell_0 is inert in K_D, which is why (D/ell_0)=1 is essential. Section 4.9 starts with an arbitrary cyclic quotient T/T'=Z/NZ, so it does not remove powers of 2 from N.

The fine conductors are (1,1). The theorem's ring-class extension factor is 1 and its changed-prime local factor is N_ell_0(0,0,0)=1. Fixing the ell_0 orientation therefore gives a one-to-one oriented correspondence. Theorem 3.1 retains that orientation. Corollary 4.1 forgets it by using conjugate pairs of embeddings; it must not be used to insert another factor 1/2. Section 4.10 identifies this adelic map with actual reduction and notes that all enhanced supersingular points are defined over F_(ell_0^2).

These primary statements replace the need to inspect Deuring's 1941 original or a secondary summary of the correspondence. They do not replace the following calculation.

### 2.2 The calculation that yields the coefficient

At norm mD, m=|h_*|, an x counted by the Schwartz sum gives the embedding sqrt(Delta_D)->x. The condition (1+x)/2 in R_2 is exactly integrality of the generator of the odd maximal quadratic order. Since O_KD is maximal, this is an optimal embedding.

At each p^e||N the split quadratic action decomposes the two lattices into integral idempotent summands. A cyclic p^e quotient cannot have a nonzero p-primary quotient in both summands, so there are two local orientations. This argument includes p=2. At ell_0 the unramified quadratic residue field has two embeddings, hence two orientations. Elsewhere there is one local orbit.

For each fixed orientation tuple the global orbit is Pic(O_KD). If x_0 and z_0 represent its initial point, the class [a] has representative a z_0 and

    (a z_0)^(-1) x_0 (a z_0)=z_0^(-1) x_0 z_0.

The Schwartz weight is therefore constant along that orbit. The remaining norm factor is precisely the unramified genus character chi_h*(Norm a). Its triviality on local unit norms at the ramified h_* places is the norm-square condition for that quadratic extension. Under the primary equivariant reduction map this class sum is the reduction of the same unaveraged P_D.

The centralizer stabilizer in R_z^times is O_KD^times={+1,-1}. Each conjugacy orbit consequently contributes |R_z^times|/2 vectors; the mass factor 1/|R_z^times| leaves exactly 1/2.

Reversing a level orientation gives alpha_p(-1) from the local weight and w_(p^e) chi_h*(p)^e from the norm-weighted value of pi. Their product is 1 by the defining parity

    alpha_p(-1)=w_(p^e) chi_h*(p)^e.

Reversing the ell_0 orientation gives -1 from the residue-line weight and chi_h*(ell_0) times the Frobenius sign from the point, also -1. Those cancel. All 2^(omega(N)+1) tuples have the same contribution. Together with the stabilizer factor this gives 2^omega(N), not an average. The initial nonzero local weight is a product of the fixed root-character values and quadratic signs, hence a 2-power root of unity. This proves (1).

This is the resolution of M1. Nothing here divides by the class number, the number of orientations, or ell_0-1.

## 3. M2, first part: the weighted adjointness

Write w_z=|R_z^times| and theta_z(n) for the local-weight lattice sum before multiplying by chi_h*(Nrd z). The product

    F_n(z)=chi_h*(Nrd z) theta_z(n)

is well-defined on the double-coset set. The inverse conjugation character of the Schwartz function cancels the right-unit change of the norm character.

Define the bilinear mass pairing

    <P,F> = sum_z P(z) F(z)/w_z.

No complex conjugation belongs here. Consider the finite groupoid of enhanced supersingular cyclic p-isogenies. For a fixed source z, summing its outgoing cyclic subgroups with weight 1/w_z is the same as summing their isomorphism classes with weight 1/|Aut(isogeny)|, by orbit-stabilizer. Dualizing the isogeny preserves its automorphism group and reverses source and target. On the adelic side the inverse neighbor double coset differs from the original one by the central idele p at p.

That central idele acts trivially on X over Q: multiply by the rational central p and absorb its units at all other finite primes into Rhat^times. It also acts trivially on F_n, since conjugation is trivial and its reduced norm is a square. Thus dualization proves the exact identity

    <T_p P,F_n>=<P,T_p F_n>.                                  (2)

This proves the missing weighted-adjointness sentence without assuming all vertex stabilizers have the same order. It is equally valid before choosing integer lifts of P_M.

For clarity, the local neighbor count used on the right side of (2) is the following. A nonzero trace-zero matrix modulo p of norm n has 1+(-n/p) invariant lines. Zero has p+1. A nonintegral contribution is x=y/p, with y primitive and Q(y)=p^2 n. Choose a basis with y mod p=[0,1;0,0]. Its upper-right entry is a unit and its diagonal entries are divisible by p. The identity Q(y)=-a^2-bc then forces p^2|c. Exactly the kernel-line neighbor contains x integrally.

With s(p)=eta_B(p), scaling outside p produces

    sum_i theta_(z g_i)(n)
       =s(p)^(-1)theta_z(p^2 n)+(-n/p)theta_z(n)
          +p s(p)theta_z(n/p^2).

Every neighbor has reduced norm p, so the factor chi_h*(p) appears in T_p F_n. Combining (2) with the actual function identity T_p P_M=a_p(E)P_M modulo 2^M gives

    H(p^2n)+s(p)(-n/p)H(n)+p s(p)^2H(n/p^2)
        =s(p)chi_h*(p)a_p(E)H(n) mod 2^M O_coef.               (3)

The c0 factor makes the mass sum integral, so lifting P_M arbitrarily to integers loses no precision. The argument does not lift its eigenrelation to characteristic zero.

For a_M(n)=H(mn), put eta=eta_B chi_h* and psi=eta_B(m/ .). Equation (3) gives O.6 exactly. Their quotient is chi_-1 on odd good integers. The recurrence is only asserted away from the full fixed support and ell_0. No extra sieve at a good prime is silently commuted through it.

## 4. M2, second part: a relative theta line at 2

The primary source is Weil, "Sur certains groupes d'operateurs unitaires", Acta Mathematica 111 (1964). I re-read the rendered pages 160, 173, 176-177 and 193. They give the multiplication, scaling and Fourier operators in I.13; multiplicativity of the index in II.25; the norm-residue identity in II.28; and rational invariance of the adelic theta sum in III, Theorem 6.

All local fields used here are Q_p, of characteristic zero. Weil's exclusion of characteristic 2 in the diagonal formula does not exclude Q_2. Residue characteristic must not be confused with field characteristic.

Let gamma(a) denote the index of a X^2. The four-dimensional norm-form identity on p.176 and multiplicativity on p.173 give

    gamma(a)gamma(b)=gamma(1)gamma(ab)(a,b).

For Q=sum_(i=1)^3 a_i X_i^2 this implies

    gamma(uQ)/gamma(Q)
      =[gamma(u)/gamma(1)]^3 (u,det Q).                       (4)

For the trace-zero reduced norm, Q has a presentation aX^2+bY^2+abZ^2, whose determinant is (ab)^2. The extra symbol in (4) is therefore 1. No Hasse invariant remains in this ratio.

The local support is compact and Q is integral on it. Hence upper unipotents n(Z_p) fix f_p. If f_p is constant on translates by L'_p, its Fourier transform is supported on the dual lattice. Choose A_p so that p^A_p Q((L'_p)^vee) is integral. Fourier conjugation then shows that lower unipotents n^-(p^A_p Z_p) fix f_p. This also works at p=2.

Here is a precise way to avoid overreading the phrase "the standard metaplectic splitting." Use the reference quadratic space Q_0=X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2 and its standard lattice vector. Its theta sum is the ordinary theta function cubed. Enlarge A_2 to at least 2 and enlarge the other A_p as needed so that both the constructed and the reference lines are stable on K_0(p^A_p). On this inverse image in the metaplectic group, each line has a scalar character. Both are genuine of the same odd rank. Therefore their ratio is trivial on the central kernel and descends to K_0(p^A_p), independently of the lift chosen for a matrix.

On upper and sufficiently deep lower unipotents this ratio is 1. On diag(u,u^-1) it is the scaling character times (u,det Q), by (4). Every matrix of K_0(p^A_p), A_p>=1, has a unit upper-left entry a and factors as

    n^-(c/a) diag(a,a^-1) n(b/a).

The quotient character is thus determined by this diagonal calculation. The relation ad=1 mod p^A_p and the chosen character depth convert it to the prescribed lower-right character, with the inverse-idele convention of CO22-27. Taking the product over places gives exactly eta_B as in (3), also for old characters of order greater than 2.

This argument uses the ordinary theta multiplier as a reference. It does not posit a canonical splitting over all SL_2(Z_2), and it does not require arbitrary chosen lifts of the three factors to multiply without a sign. The sign cancels in the ratio of the two genuine line characters. The reference theta multiplier itself follows from the one-dimensional Poisson formula.

Weil's rational-invariance theorem and the positive Gaussian at infinity now produce a genuine automorphic theta function with weight 3/2. The finite Schwartz function is a finite union of lattice cosets, so the theta sum converges normally on compact subsets. At every cusp the finite Weil transform remains Schwartz; the same positive quadratic form has only nonnegative rational exponents. These facts establish holomorphy, a conclusion not stated by the rational-invariance theorem alone.

For coefficient extraction, use Q/m on the same vector space and the same finite functions. At every p|m the support has v_p(Q)=1, so Q/m is integral there, and it is integral on the support at all other primes. The preceding argument applies directly. Its determinant squareclass is m; (4) adds (m/ .), yielding psi in O.5. There is no unsupported half-integral-weight U_m assertion.

## 5. The remaining named classical inputs

### 5.1 Cusp constants and one fixed L

I obtained and read Elkik's complete primary proof, "Le theoreme de Manin-Drinfeld", Asterisque 183 (1990), pp.59-67. The theorem on p.59 applies to every congruence subgroup and every degree-zero divisor supported on its cusps. The proof on pp.64-66 separates the p+1 cusp eigenvalue from the strictly smaller absolute eigenvalues on the cusp-form part, after choosing p=1 modulo the level. Thus the relevant cusp divisor is torsion. No squarefree-level hypothesis is present.

Apply it to W_(p^e)(infinity)-infinity on X_0(N). There are finitely many such divisors for the fixed N. Their images in E are torsion, so one integer kills all their images. This theorem is sufficient even if individual cusps are not rational. It is the exact assertion needed for the translation constants in pi composed with W_(p^e).

The bounded-degree torsion factor in L is a separate elementary argument. Choose two distinct good primes p,q of the fixed E. Prime-to-p torsion over a field of degree at most four injects into E(F_(p^f)) for some f<=4; use q for the p-primary part. The product of the finitely many group orders kills the relevant torsion uniformly in D. A fixed further factor clears the quadratic projectors. Nothing here depends on ell_0 or M. Elkik supplies a primary proof of the needed Manin-Drinfeld theorem, so the original Manin and Drinfeld articles need not be separately used as unchecked evidence.

### 5.3 The actual Kummer stages

Serre's 1972 primary pp.259-260 were viewed again. For a non-CM curve over a number field the Tate image is open, including at 2. Here non-CM follows from the nonintegral rational j-invariant. Thus the image contains 1+2^n M_2(Z_2), and in particular a nonidentity central scalar. This is a characteristic-zero argument, not residual irreducibility.

For the rational nontorsion point Q on A=E^(D_w), let c_Q be its integral division cocycle and H the kernel of the Tate representation. Its image on H is a closed Z_2-submodule. If its rational span were zero, c_Q would descend to the Tate image; the central-scalar identity

    (z-1)c(g)=(g-1)c(z)

would make its rational class a coboundary. Clearing one denominator contradicts Kummer injectivity and Mordell-Weil. Openness makes the nonzero invariant rational span all of V_2A. The image therefore contains a full lattice. Intersecting H with a fixed finite-index kernel retains a full lattice.

Choose one actual complex conjugation tau, one nonzero integral row b with bC=b, and h in that fixed kernel with v=c_Q(h), b(v) nonzero. Then sigma=h tau is an actual element with

    c_Q(sigma^2)=(1+C)v,       b c_Q(sigma^2)=2b(v).

All fixed field restrictions are restrictions of sigma itself. Chebotarev is applied to its image in the joint finite quotient of torsion, division points and those fixed fields. This realizes compatible prescriptions, not a Cartesian product of unrelated conjugacy classes. At each sufficiently large M it supplies ell_0 with the fixed nonzero valuation c_w=v_2(2b(v)). The same chosen place identifies the torsion matrix, point reduction and row b.

Finite-field Kummer is evaluated at Fr_ell_0^2. Since that matrix is 1 modulo 2^M, and Fr^2-1 is a surjective isogeny on the algebraic closure, the map has target T/2^M. It is integral and has the required Frobenius sign. Later finite exclusions discard primes but do not alter sigma or c_w. No assertion is made that c_w stays bounded under arbitrary growing auxiliary extensions or at all primes satisfying only the torsion congruence.

This supplies the exact co:kummer output on the non-CM family. The optional broader CM formulation in the raw lemma is not used or approved here.

## 6. Precision, nonvanishing, and immediate consumers

Reduction modulo 2^M means modulo that integer ideal in the fixed coefficient ring. Since each R_z^times injects into GL_4(F_3), every c0/w_z is an integer. The changing size of the class set causes no denominator. The moving ell_0 weights are quadratic, so the coefficient field is generated by old alpha_p values alone.

At D_w the support condition holds by construction. Equation (1) has right-side valuation c_w+9+omega(N). For M greater than that number, a_M(D_w) has exactly that finite valuation. This preserves the same visible witness throughout an arbitrarily advanced retained sequence.

For a simple-zero D, write the actual native point as m_D G_D+torsion. The same L kills its torsion. Integral reduction and lambda_M cannot lower v_2(m_D). The rational-to-native rank-one free-line index divides 2 by restriction and norm. These facts give the fixed-convention O.8 inequality. If the odd twist is not a simple zero, the same primary height formula makes P_D torsion; L kills it and its coefficient is zero modulo 2^M. If (D/ell_0) is not 1 its coefficient is already zero by support. Thus every visible coefficient remains in the domain of the point comparison and forces a simple zero.

This checks the immediate coefficient and visible-witness consumers. The later requirement that graph products remain in the moving support was audited separately. This report does not replace that graph audit.
