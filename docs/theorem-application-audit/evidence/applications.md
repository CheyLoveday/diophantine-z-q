## 2. Kato 13.4(2): every hypothesis on the actual object

The target is the fixed non-CM curve E_ell, not an arbitrary elliptic curve. Its j-invariant is 35152/9, hence is not an algebraic integer and cannot be a CM j-invariant. All quadratic twists remain non-CM. The representation is the actual rank-two Tate module, after the one fixed comparison from the original modular form. The representation is not replaced by its reduction modulo two.

## 3. Fixed normalization, ramified characters, and exceptional Euler zeros

The smoothing and character conventions are checked on the printed formulas, not inferred from an L-value slogan. At k=2 and r=r'=1, both alternatives of 4.2.4 give (u,v)=(1,1). The retained version of 6.6 is a sum over all residues, with no division by the number of residues. It uses f* and the inverse character on the analytic side. For the fixed rational newform f*=f; auxiliary cyclotomic characters still retain their inverse. Theorem 9.7 sends the very same smoothed etale class to this modular-form expression. See printed pp.143,163,189, and E 18–49,77–91.

The smoothing integers are odd and congruent to 1 modulo the odd support and to 1+2^e modulo 2^(e+1), for fixed e≥max(3,v_2N). They are therefore prime to 6 and the class conductor, and satisfy the original congruence modulo N. At the quadratic central constituent their character values are 1, so each factor c^2−c has valuation e. Their product has valuation 2e even if their numerical sizes increase with the support. Nothing is divided by a vanishing smoothing factor. For a finite-order nonquadratic character, c−chi(c) cannot be zero for c>1, because chi(c) is a root of unity; this also checks the generic-nonvanishing application.

For an odd signed squarefree h with discriminant Delta_h=4h, omitting the dyadic character would change the representation. The actual basis map E.2 includes both character factors at every sufficiently high cyclotomic level. If a quadratic character is nontrivial on Gamma, the action is changed by t↦−2−t. The comparison with the full-conductor transfer is cofinal: introducing more powers of a prime already in the level has no Euler factor in 8.12. Thus the comparison is an equality of the retained classes, not an equality of just their central L-values. For odd h the ramified dyadic possibilities are a finite list; in fact the quadratic character of conductor four is on the real-sign factor, while the displayed Gamma automorphism also covers the allowed conductor-eight enlargement. The signed Gauss relation is tau(chi_h)^2=Delta_h. Replacing its right side by chi_h(−1)Delta_h would double-count the sign.

At the old support the local types of a quadratic twist form a finite set. Local Tate duality identifies H^2(Q_v,V_h) with the dual of rational Tate invariants, which vanish because local elliptic torsion is finite. Local Iwasawa control therefore excludes t from the finite local clearing factor. Taking the product over those finite types gives one F_E with F_E(0)≠0. This is the justification for using F_E; nonzero generic rank alone would not show its central value nonzero.

At a new active odd prime, inertia acts through the nontrivial quadratic sign; over Q_2 its invariants and the relevant rational inertia cohomology vanish. At an unused prime, the rational j_*-to-full cone is the two-term Frobenius complex on H^1(I_q,V_h), in degrees one and two. Its differential is generically invertible, hence injective over the one-variable domain even when its determinant has a zero at a height-one prime. Its degree-two length is precisely the order of that determinant. The same Euler factor occurs in the transferred class E.1, so it pays this length. At bad primes H^1(I_q,V_h) must be used; replacing it indiscriminately by V_h^{I_q}(−1) is not justified.

This addresses exceptional characters: a specialization may make an unused Euler factor zero, and a central analytic value may vanish. Neither is discarded as an exceptional set in the divisibility proof. The generic identity is cleared before specialization. The numerical central identity E.7–E.9 is used only where Selmer corank is zero; its finite nonzero valuation form additionally assumes the central L-value is nonzero. A rank-one curve with finite Sha would be a counterexample to treating finite Sha alone as that corank-zero hypothesis. The actual unknown base has full corank one and does not enter this central Kato calculation. Known nonzero central values get the required corank-zero conclusion from the separate forward theorem.

The integral positive-complex computation and central saturation cancellation are project arguments, not conclusions of Kato 13.4. This audit does not relabel them as published theorems. In particular primitivity after inverting t is never specialized to t=0; the central saturation index is retained and cancels in E 195–215.

## 4. The even theta source: existence before normalization

The data are a sign sigma, old finite support S_0 containing 2N, and a positive squarefree reference D_0 in prescribed unit squareclasses with L_*(sigma D_0)≠0. The asserted form may depend on this data. It is not a single form for all unrelated local squareclasses.

Waldspurger's Proposition 18 is used through its actual proof, printed p.467: nonzero central L-value gives a nonzero lift and a nonzero Fourier functional at the specified index in the corresponding packet. The index must be locally admissible for that packet. Here the packet is selected by that same nonzero Fourier functional (equivalently the nonzero lift for the additive character at D_0), and the positive D_0 selects the holomorphic real member. One is not taking an arbitrary packet member and asserting that it has every nonzero coefficient. The ICM Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 identify this nonzero global lift as a cuspidal irreducible constituent orthogonal to elementary unary theta constituents. The local transformations used next remain inside it.

The remaining selection is a direct local argument, not an extra assertion of Proposition 18. For an old p let ell_{D_0}(v)≠0. The additive projection P_R retains that functional. Applying a unit u after projection kills its D_0 functional unless u^2≡1 modulo p^R, because the Whittaker index scales by u^2. For odd p and large R the surviving set is the two small neighborhoods of ±1. At p=2 it is the neighborhoods ±1+2^(R−1)Z_2; the four residues modulo 2^R are not four independent signs. Smoothness makes the coefficient locally constant there. Choose a finite character alpha of two-power order whose value at −1 matches the central action. Such a character exists on the 2-primary quotient of Z_p^×. The two contributions to the alpha-average then agree and are nonzero. Deep lower-unipotent invariance follows by smoothness and compactness of the finite projection. This yields a vector with a scalar K_0 character and with its original D_0 functional nonzero.

All these changes occur at S_0. At p outside S_0 the spherical local vector can be retained even if p divides D_0. Printed pp.377–378 state that its coefficient c_p^0 is one at squarefree valuation zero **and** one. Thus no prime divisor of D_0 needs to be added to the level. This detail is necessary for comparing D and D_0 at a common S_0-supported level. Finite additive projections impose the prescribed unit-squareclass support without killing D_0. The resulting scalar character has two-power order and the global parity required for the holomorphic form.

H1 is also checked in the correct order. At printed p.377 Waldspurger defines the full almost-all-good-Hecke space and proves in Proposition 2 that its nonzero existence is equivalent to H1. We have already constructed a nonzero member in that space; consequently its pair (phi_0,psi) satisfies H1. H1 is not being assumed for every twist/character pair. H2 is not needed for Corollary 2 when the two indices lie in the same Q_2 squareclass, as the author expressly explains on printed p.484.

## 5. Full-space proportionality, rational phase, and the late sieve

For E 283–300 the packet data are phi_0=f_E tensor eta, chi_0=psi chi_{−1}, and eta=psi chi_{−sigma}; hence phi_0 tensor chi_0^(−1)=f_E tensor chi_sigma. These are the analytic factors in the printed formula. The ratio D/D_0 is a square at **every** prime dividing the actual auxiliary level, since that level is supported on S_0 and both parameters have the same prescribed unit classes there. Its positivity handles the real place.

Corollary 2 is expressly for every f in the full almost-all-Hecke space. Its proof on p.483 explicitly extends the linear coefficient relation to the full space before squaring. Thus old multiplicities are included. With n_1=D_0,n_2=D, the character factor is psi(D/D_0), the orientation in E.10. Normalizing the square-root size in L_* accounts for the weight-3/2 power of D/D_0. Polarization is legitimate over characteristic zero: two linear functionals whose squares are proportional are proportional. When the analytic ratio is zero the D-functional is zero. When it is nonzero its proportionality factor is fixed on the whole space; there is no arbitrary sign for each chosen vector.

Ramsey supplies an algebraic finite-level section space and diamond action. Its base ring in Definition 3.1 inverts 4N; the q-expansion statement 4.2 is a descent statement in that setting. Only flat extension between coefficient fields is needed to choose an algebraic vector with a nonzero D_0 coefficient and then normalize it to one. Galois sends the chosen Tate cusp to its diamond translate, so coefficientwise conjugation stays at the same level with conjugate character after that diamond adjustment. Twisting by a finite character is a finite translation construction; it raises the level only at that character's support and changes the scalar character by its square.

Consequently if tau acts on the relevant roots of unity by an odd power e, g^tau and g tensor nu, nu=eta^((e−1)/2), lie in the same full space at a common S_0-supported level. Their D_0 coefficients are 1 and nu(D_0). Full-space proportionality gives tau(a(D))=nu(D/D_0)a(D). For compatible local square roots of D/D_0, let z_D be their eta-value. Then z_D^2=eta(D/D_0)=psi(D/D_0), since the quadratic chi_{−sigma} is one on these local squares. Moreover tau(z_D)/z_D=nu(D/D_0). It follows that a(D)/z_D is fixed by every algebraic automorphism and belongs to Q. This proves integer-valued 2-adic valuations of the nonzero normalized coefficients, including the choice needed to attain an actual integer minimum.

The extra exclusion set B is imposed only after this comparison. The explicit hypothesis gcd(D_0,product B)=1 prevents the sieve from killing the reference coefficient. No full-space comparison is made at a new B-prime where D/D_0 might not be a square. Recurrence primes subsequently avoid S_0 union B. A sieve may make a previously nonzero coefficient vanish if its index meets B; this is intended, and cannot be treated as preservation of all coefficients. Later graph choices use the already fixed minimum and do not rerun this normalization.

Concrete failed variants are: compare across different dyadic squareclasses while dropping H2; add a new level prime without fixing its local ratio; use a vector with zero D_0 coefficient and divide by it; or infer arbitrary mod-two base change from Ramsey 4.3. The latter theorem starts at weight at least 5/2, and Remark 4.4 explicitly notes the 3/2 limitation. None is a valid substitute for the current construction.

## 6. Fixed odd witness and visibility: precise external hypotheses

BFH's opening theorem requires a cuspidal even-weight newform of trivial character and a finite set containing every level prime. The newform of E has these properties. Include two and every extra fixed avoidance prime. For root number epsilon=−1 its first clause supplies a **positive** fundamental discriminant with simple zero, and its second supplies a **negative** fundamental discriminant with nonzero central value. Applying the second first fixes h_*, and adding its primes before the first fixes D_w coprime to h_*. Splitting at two gives the required odd discriminants and unit class. Finite exceptions can be removed by adding their prime divisors to the finite set; the theorem is applied anew to a finite set, not asserted uniformly with an unproved analytic threshold. The parity theorem producing epsilon=−1 is a distinct input, not a consequence of BFH or the full-corank-one hypothesis by itself.

CST 1.1, printed pp.2524–2525, requires a normalized weight-two newform, imaginary quadratic K, a primitive ring-class character of conductor c, gcd(c,N)=1, no inert prime dividing N, splitting when p^2 divides N, and a special condition at primes dividing both N and disc K. Here K_D=Q(sqrt(Dh_*)), D is in the entire admissible odd filter, all primes of 2N split, gcd(Dh_*,N)=1, and c=1. Thus the final special condition is vacuous, even if N has prime powers or a dyadic power. Every class-group character has ring conductor one in this setting; the genus character is quadratic, including its trivial possible boundary. The point is the literal class sum in CST, with pi(infinity)=O, not the sum divided by its class number. The unit factor is one because the admissible discriminants exclude −3 and −4. The fixed parametrization contributes its fixed degree.

The genus Rankin L-series is L(E^D,s)L(E^{h_*},s). The second factor has fixed nonzero central value. The admissible positive D has the same odd root sign as E, so L(E^D,1)=0 before differentiating the product. Therefore a nontorsion genus point is equivalent here to nonzero derivative of L(E^D,s), by the displayed height identity and positive definiteness of the height on the free group. It cannot establish a simple zero for arbitrary local types or with a zero companion. The unwanted component lies on the fixed rank-zero twist and is torsion. The genus character makes the point defined over a field of degree at most four over Q; local reduction at two fixed distinct good primes bounds all its torsion, so one L kills it for every admissible D. The rational/native free-lattice comparison has bounded index independently of D. These are the reasons the same L can be used in the visibility test.

For the weighted coefficient comparison at a stage ell_0, impose (D/ell_0)=1. Then ell_0 is inert in K_D, prime to N and the discriminant. In Cornut–Jetchev take F=Q, B=M_2(Q), S={ell_0}; H.1–H.3 hold since the changed prime was split in B, 1+0+1 is even, and it is inert in K_D. The Eichler level is prime to ell_0 but can contain powers of old primes. Both fine conductors are one. In J 575–598 the factor kappa is the ring-class ratio times the local counting factor; each equals one. Sections 4.9–4.10 identify the adelic map with actual modular reduction. This directly covers the modular level structure, not just a set of unenhanced supersingular curves.

There are two old cyclic-level orientations at every p^e dividing N, since the local algebra splits and a cyclic quotient cannot have nonzero pieces in both idempotent summands. The changed-prime orientations are also retained. The embedding stabilizer consists of the units of the quadratic order, namely ±1. Thus the weighted orbit contributes 1/2; combining all 2^(omega(N)+1) orientations gives the factor 2^omega(N) in O.7. The local point and weight change by the same sign under each reversal. Omitting stabilizer weights would fail for supersingular curves with extra automorphisms, although the quadratic order itself has no extra units.

If D is nonsplit at ell_0, including ell_0 dividing D, this theorem is not applied: the actual local Schwartz factor is zero, as O 236–239 states. This boundary is needed for the all-admissible-D claim. On the permitted support, nonzero coefficient modulo 2^M implies LP_D is not torsion. CST then gives a simple zero. Only after that conclusion is the native rank-one index used. If analytic rank jumps above one, CST gives a torsion point and the same L kills it, so the coefficient vanishes. No index of a torsion point is invented.

## 7. Bounded-prime companions and uniformity

Hoffstein–Luo requires a normalized holomorphic newform of even weight at least two and trivial nebentypus. Every quadratic twist of E has such a form. The theorem permits any finite splitting set and supplies infinitely many odd fundamental discriminants with at most four prime factors. This is an absolute count; no uniform bound on discriminant size or on the analytic threshold is needed.

For the rough companion used in N, the current curve may vary with the parameter h and a bounded dummy product a. Choose a signed new prime q_0 so that a q_0 is a negative fundamental square at the required old local places. This makes A=E^(h a q_0) have positive root number. Apply Hoffstein–Luo to A, with the primes of its conductor, 2, and every required finite avoidance set. Its d is positive: the splitting conditions give chi_d(N_A)=1, while nonzero central value forces w(A)chi_d(−N_A)=+1. Thus sign(d)=+1. The product k'=a q_0 d has at most omega(a)+5 prime factors and the desired nonzero central value. This argument works even when the original signed odd parameter has field discriminant 4h. It does not enlarge the odd D-filter of the theta comparison to an unproved dyadic class.

The finite avoidance set is allowed to depend on h. What must stay uniform is the prime-count bound, and that is the literal four in the theorem plus the one preparatory q_0 and the already bounded dummy count. Excluding finitely many exceptional fields from infinitely many d has no additional prime cost. BFH alone would not give this count. Hoffstein–Luo's subsequent remark about three prime factors explicitly omits a proof and is not used.

These checks preserve the order of constants: the normalized source and its minimum are fixed first; the bounded-prime argument proves the required inequality without changing that source; the final negative unit fixes K; then the resulting B is fixed before determinant precision, Boolean dimension, graph primes, and the selected finite theta stage. The early empty odd-unit branch already proves RC and needs no B. Nothing here asserts a two-nonempty-seed family on that early branch.

## 8. The two concrete report-level failures and their correction

The old RC report incorrectly wrote v_q=P_delta/P_e^2. That would make inactive evaluation 1+2P_delta/P_e^2 and would not cancel the support determinant. The correct raw/current definition is

    v_q = (l_q/e_q^2 − 1)/2,
    eta_q = 1 + v_q(1+g_q).

Here e_q=a_q−z−z^(−1), while l_q is the product of the two inverse-Frobenius determinants. Modulo two,

    (1+a_q z+z^2)(1+a_q z^(−1)+z^(−2))
      = (a_q+z+z^(−1))^2.

The relevant residual series are nonzero because z=(1+t)^m with m odd, so e_q and l_q are units in the O-group ring. The quotient difference is divisible by two there. At an active character eta_q=1; at an inactive one eta_q=l_q/e_q^2. The actual support comparison is l_q u_rel=e_q^2 epsilon_q u_prim with epsilon_q in Lambda^×. Therefore the corrected product equals epsilon_q u_prim in the fraction field and has its regular extension at every zero. For example a_q=2 can make e_q(0)=0; the identity supplies exactly the cancelling zero in u_rel. It would be wrong to specialize eta_q separately. See RD 1316–1326 and A 310–325; the additive correction gives the complete typed comparison.

The second issue is a missing justification in the old report's sentence about a central zero class. A nonzero generic class paired with a functional having a compensating pole could specialize to a nonzero scalar. The required remedy is stronger than generic rank: over Lambda_(t), active quadratic inertia has differential −2, hence is acyclic, and the moving-prime Frobenius determinants are units because their central a_r±2 valuations are fixed and finite. The old Kummer conditions carry exact orthogonal duality. Thus the actual orthogonal-to-relaxed comparison has a homotopy inverse over Lambda_(t). Apply it to the conjugate actual class and then the retained duality map. This constructs a closed **regular Lambda_(t)-functional** whose generic fiber is the functional used in the coordinate. The inverse is unique in the derived category, so this comparison does not choose a new normalization. The local argument does not require a nonzero central point or finite Sha. At the unknown base there are no active primes, and the same moving-prime argument applies. A central zero class then pairs to zero. RD 439–464 and 1062–1072 provide the construction and the explicit regular representative; the additive correction separates it from the surrounding simple-zero numerical application.

## 9. Disposition and remaining scope

No failed application of the inspected published Kato, Waldspurger, Ramsey, BFH, Hoffstein–Luo, or CST statements was found in their current domains. The actual Cornut–Jetchev theorem and proof used by the project were checked in its identified author versions; the unavailable publisher PDF is a version-binding limitation, not a missing statement in the inspected primary text. The two concrete failures in the earlier RC report are handled by its separately pinned correction; they must not disappear behind the earlier conclusion.

This result does not convert the project constructions into external theorems. The integral positive complex, all-sequence evaluation/derivative argument, outer-limit continuity, finite graph construction, common functional, and final determinant convolution still require their actual separate proofs.
