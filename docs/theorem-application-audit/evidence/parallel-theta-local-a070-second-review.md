## The finite group and its field of definition

Let C be the subgroup of J_0(N)(Qbar) generated by [c]-[infinity] as c ranges over all geometric cusps. There are finitely many geometric cusps. Fix an embedding Qbar into C. Elkik's theorem says every listed generator is torsion in the complex Jacobian. The map from Qbar-valued points into complex points is injective, so the same torsion equality holds over Qbar. If m_c kills the c-th generator, the product, or least common multiple, of the finitely many m_c kills C. Thus C is a finite group, not merely a torsion subgroup with potentially unbounded exponent.

The point infinity is rational, the entire cusp set is Galois-stable, and phi is defined over Q. Therefore C and phi(C) are Galois-stable finite subgroups. No individual cusp needs to be rational over Q, over the Heegner field, or over the finite field used for a theta stage. A positive integer C_pi equal to the exponent of phi(C) is an ordinary rational integer and kills every geometric point of phi(C), hence also every point of this group over every extension field. This construction depends only on fixed N and fixed phi.

This is stronger than bounding E(F)_tors for fields F of degree at most four. The separate B in lines 7966–7984 serves that latter purpose. It is not used to bound cusp-image fields. Setting B_cusp=C_pi avoids conflating those two arguments.

## Good Hecke correspondence and the actual target

Write i_infinity(x)=[x]-[infinity] and phi=alpha q_f. The point map is pi=phi i_infinity. At q not dividing N, T_q on divisors is the sum of q+1 isogeny neighbors, with their usual multiplicities. Its standard upper-triangular representatives send infinity to infinity, hence T_q[infinity]=(q+1)[infinity]. The correspondence used on J_0(N) is the same divisor correspondence. Its action on the f-quotient is [a_q(f)], and alpha commutes with the integer multiplication [a_q(f)]. Consequently

sum_{neighbors x'} pi(x')
=phi(T_q[x]-(q+1)[infinity])
=phi(T_q([x]-[infinity]))
=[a_q(f)]pi(x).

This is exact equality of algebraic points. There is no residual cusp correction to this good-prime relation and no division by q+1. It applies to the unaveraged trace relations around (4.1). The good-Hecke formula is not a claim that pi itself is a group homomorphism from the modular curve.

One can also verify quotient equivariance by differentials. For a nonzero invariant differential omega_E, pi^*omega_E=c f(z)dz for a fixed nonzero constant c. On the chosen f-quotient, the dual of T_q acts by a_q. Thus the difference homomorphism phi T_q-[a_q]phi has zero pullback on differentials. In characteristic zero a nonzero homomorphism from an abelian variety to an elliptic curve is surjective and has nonzero differential. Hence the difference homomorphism is zero. This argument is on actual characteristic-zero homomorphisms, not only in a rational Hom group.

## Atkin–Lehner normalization and the translation term

For an exact divisor m=p^e of N and trivial nebentypus, take the usual rational Atkin–Lehner involution W_m of X_0(N). Let w_m be its eigenvalue on the weight-two newform, as used by the manuscript. In the standard determinant-normalized weight-two slash operation,

(f|_2 gamma)(z)=det(gamma)(cz+d)^(-2) f(gamma z).

This is exactly the coefficient of the pullback of f(z)dz, because d(gamma z)=det(gamma)(cz+d)^(-2) dz. Therefore W_m^*(f(z)dz)=w_m f(z)dz. There is no additional p^e factor when passing from this slash convention to differentials. The involution property gives w_m in {+1,-1} on this rational newform line.

The induced Jacobian map W_{m,*} sends [x]-[infinity] to [W_m x]-[W_m infinity]. The differential argument above gives phi W_{m,*}=[w_m]phi on the actual target. Hence

pi(W_m x)=[w_m]pi(x)+pi(W_m infinity).

The last term is phi([W_m infinity]-[infinity]) and belongs to phi(C). Normalization pi(infinity)=0 does not set this different cusp image to zero. Multiplication by C_pi kills it exactly. Products of Atkin–Lehner operators cause no new problem: every resulting cusp difference still belongs to the same C, or the translations add as integer linear combinations of the already annihilated values. The manuscript's norm-character signs in the theta weighting are separate from this cusp correction; this review verifies only the parametrization contribution w_m.

## A066: actual-target check

The map alpha:A_f to E is a Q-isogeny. Its existence is justified by the common modular L-series and the isogeny theorem; choosing its dual if the initial isogeny is oriented the other way supplies the requested direction. q_f is nonzero and onto A_f, so phi=alpha q_f is nonzero and onto E. Its composition with the based Abel–Jacobi map is nonconstant, since the image of the curve generates its Jacobian. The construction thus supplies the actual target E, not just an unspecified isogenous curve.

For Hecke and Atkin–Lehner operators, alpha commutes with the integer scalar eigenvalues. The operator equality survives composition unchanged. The pullback differential is multiplied by a fixed nonzero scalar, which cancels out of the eigenvalue equations. There is no requirement that the modular degree or deg(alpha) be a 2-adic unit, and no identification of the integral Tate lattices is used. Fixed lattice indices can be retained by the existing fixed-multiplier convention.

This is a bounded check of the transport to E and its operator identities. It does not independently reconstruct the general Shimura quotient theorem or validate the r_5 realization at the separate arbitrary-coefficient use. The author-version disclosure in the proposed BCDT bibliography remains necessary.
