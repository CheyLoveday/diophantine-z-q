## A023: rational Euler-system divisibility

Registered uses are C024, lines 1736 through 1744, and context anchors 1734 through 1738, 1745 through 1749, 6907 through 6911 and 7036 through 7040. The actual discharge is expanded in paragraphs 7 and 8 of the Kato appendix, lines 6899 through 7041.

The object is the fixed covariant Tate lattice T=T_2E, quadratically twisted, with cyclotomic inverse deck action. The ring after rationalization is Z_2[[t]][1/2], with finite real components treated separately. This is the prime-to-two height-one application of Theorem 13.4(2). It is not part (3), and needs neither residual irreducibility nor p unequal to two.

The five hypotheses are discharged as follows. The retained system is generically nonzero on both real components because one fixed symbol combination has nonzero projections on both Betti lines; Theorems 6.6 and 9.7 and 13.5(2) give infinitely many nonzero finite-character specializations on each component. Smoothing factors c^2-c rho(c)^(-1) and d^2-d rho(d)^(-1) cannot vanish since c,d>1 and rho values are roots of unity. A nonzero integral power series, with only bounded powers of two inverted, has finitely many cyclotomic zeros. Thus a torsion class cannot explain these specializations. Complex conjugation on a covariant elliptic Tate module has two eigenspaces of rank one, also after quadratic twisting. Inverse arithmetic Frobenius has weight -1 here, as required by the source's purity convention. The fixed non-CM open image gives rational irreducibility. Its intersection with the cyclotomic kernel and the quadratic-character kernel contains an open subgroup of SL_2(Z_2), hence a nonidentity unipotent with a one-dimensional fixed space.

Theorem 13.4(2) bounds H2_0=ker(H2->H2_loc), not unrestricted H2. The current proof retains exactly this kernel in (K15). At a height-one prime the rank-one H1 module is free: the Euler characteristic gives rank one, and a hypothetical scalar torsion class would give invariants after specialization; the nonabelian open subgroup has no such invariants. Thus the source ideal J generated by all linear evaluations of the supplied class becomes its coordinate ideal in a free rank-one module. No arbitrary change of Euler-system generator is made.

The passage to full H2 is a separate current argument, correctly marked as such. The local cone uses W_q=H1(I_q,V chi), not V^I blindly at bad primes. Its determinant is the same unused-prime factor from the norm relation at good inactive primes. Ramified good quadratic inertia has zero rational cohomology. The finitely many old bad and dyadic types give a fixed F_E; local control and local duality exclude a central zero of that factor because local elliptic torsion is finite. The explicit length sum at lines 7014 onward pays once for H2_loc, once for old bad terms and once for unused primes. It introduces no growing contribution at active primes.

Downstream assumptions retained: fixed E is non-CM and modular; the integral system and its reciprocity are those validated in A049 through A051; ordinary local duality and derived local control apply to the stated rational complexes. The unrelated positive integral model and determinant arguments later used in the manuscript are not conclusions of Kato 13.4.

## A024 and A026: even nonvanishing and the root-number filter

A024 covers C025, lines 1833 through 1844, C099, lines 9617 through 9621, and context anchors 1832 through 1836, 1845 through 1849, 9608 through 9612 and 9653 through 9657. The expanded second proof is at lines 9623 through 9655. A026 covers C027, lines 1846 through 1856, and contexts 1846 through 1850 and 1854 through 1858.

A nonempty signed unit filter gives compatible unit residue classes. CRT and Dirichlet produce one signed prime q0 in those classes, outside S and the finite exclusion set B. Apply HL to the actual modular elliptic curve A^(q0), not to the original form with an unsupported class prescription. Its normalized weight-two newform has trivial nebentypus. Include all conductor primes, S, B and |q0| in the finite splitting set permitted by HL. The theorem supplies an odd fundamental discriminant d with at most four prime factors and nonzero central value. The functional equation and conductor splitting force d>0 because the intermediate curve has root number +1. Splitting at two gives d=1 modulo eight. Hence q0d has precisely the original real and finite classes, avoids B, is squarefree and has at most five distinct prime factors. No size bound for the selected primes is asserted. No use is made of HL's three-prime remark.

The root-number reduction uses local isomorphism at the fixed places, including two. At a newly ramified prime outside S the curve has good reduction and p>=5 since S contains 2 and 3. Desjardins 3.3(2) gives W_p(E^u)=(-1/p). The product over these primes is determined by the sign of u and its unit class modulo four; the old factors are fixed by the prescribed local isomorphism classes. Thus the root number is constant on the stated filter, including odd parameters 3 or 7 modulo eight whose fundamental field discriminants are 4u. No formula restricted to odd fundamental discriminants is applied to 4u.

The wording 'good odd prime newly ramified' is safe in its present context because such a prime is outside S. For extra clarity one may add 'outside S, hence p>=5'; this is optional, not a missing hypothesis in the current proof. Dependencies retained are modularity of the actual quadratic twist and the ordinary Dirichlet theorem. The construction does not impose a nonabelian Frobenius condition on d.

## A025: odd nonvanishing and the fixed genus partner

C026 is at lines 1833 through 1844; C077 is at lines 7914 through 7951. Context anchors are 1832 through 1836, 1845 through 1849, 7935 through 7939 and 7958 through 7962. The actual BFH choices are displayed at lines 7938 through 7949.

BFH applies to a cusp newform of even weight, trivial character, and a finite splitting set containing all level primes. Its signs are epsilon D<0 for the simple-zero clause (i), epsilon D>0 for nonvanishing clause (ii). In an arbitrary odd signed filter the same q0 reduction as A024 gives an intermediate curve with epsilon=-1. Clause (i) consequently gives positive d, so multiplying by q0 preserves the original sign and unit filter.

For the genus witness, the full-Selmer parity theorem first supplies epsilon(E)=-1. Clause (ii) then gives a negative fundamental h_* with L(E^(h_*),1) nonzero. Splitting at two makes h_* odd and congruent to one modulo eight; it also excludes -3 and -4. Add its prime divisors to the splitting set before applying clause (i), which gives a positive fundamental D_w with simple zero and coprime to h_*. These choices are completed before detecting primes and theta precision vary. There is no assertion that a later arbitrary nonabelian field prescription preserves this witness. The later use of the h_* component's rank zero is a separate forward theorem, whose exact citation repair is already provided in this directory.

Acceptance retains the full-Selmer parity assumption as a dependency; ordinary rational rank parity alone would not discharge it. BFH supplies neither bounded prime support nor a rank statement by itself.

## A027: the two explicit height applications

C028 is at lines 2270 through 2292; C078 is at lines 7953 through 7964. Context anchors are 2265 through 2269, 2298 through 2302, 7959 through 7963 and 7995 through 7999. The final context concerns the later integral descent of the point; CST itself is used before that descent.

CST defines P_chi as the unaveraged sum over Pic(O_c) of [P_a-infinity] times chi([a]). Its elliptic version requires the actual fixed map pi:X0(N)->E with pi(infinity)=O and divides the height by deg(pi). It states

L'(1,E,chi)=2^(-mu(N,D)) 8 pi^2 (f,f)/(u^2 c sqrt(|D|)) times h_K(P_chi(pi))/deg(pi).

All level primes split in the current applications, so both Heegner conditions hold, gcd(N,D)=1 and mu=0. Conductors are prime to N and the extra-unit fields are excluded, so u=1. The same map, degree and Petersson convention occur in both formulas. These facts avoid any unknown class-number factor or averaging factor.

For the genus application K=Q(sqrt(h h_*)), c=1, and the conductor-one genus character cuts out Q(sqrt(h),sqrt(h_*)). The Rankin product is L(E^h,s)L(E^(h_*),s). For the distinct ring-class application K'=Q(sqrt(k')), c=|h|, and restriction of the h-character has this exact conductor because h is squarefree and coprime to k'. Its Rankin product is L(E^h,s)L(E^(h k'),s). The prior companion construction supplies k' negative odd fundamental, a square at primes dividing 2Nh and disjoint from h_*; h is in the positive one-mod-eight filter and is assumed already to have a simple zero. Thus these are distinct native points, not an identification of their lattices.

Cancel the common nonzero derivative L'(E^h,1) only at these known simple-zero parameters. The ratio of the conductor/discriminant factors is |h| sqrt(|k'|)/sqrt(|h h_*|). Together with the fixed real-period definition this gives exactly the ratio L_*(h k')/L_*(h_*). Forward rank one for E^h and rank zero for the companion components identify both native rational free lines. The norm makes twice a conjugation-invariant free class rational, so each native free line has index at most two relative to its rational line. Heights square these indices. The error in the difference of their 2-adic height valuations therefore has absolute value at most two, as in (3.4). Torsion and fixed period choices do not introduce a variable factor.

At D_w the same conductor-one formula has nonzero Rankin derivative L'(E^(D_w),1)L(E^(h_*),1), so the actual unaveraged point is nontorsion. Its later fixed integral projection to the D component is a separate Galois/Kummer argument, not an assertion that the initial point is E(K_D)-rational. The theorem's use is valid also when the general genus construction permits D=1; the witness itself is supplied by BFH.

Dependencies retained: actual normalized modular parametrization, the admissible companion construction, forward rank-zero/rank-one theorems and the elementary free-line norm argument. This application does not prove the arithmetic lower estimate (3.5), the theta detection theorem or the subsequent limit argument.

Smoother primes exclude Euler-system indices, but are not added to the arithmetic support in Kato 8.1.3/8.2. Unused primes in F/d are good since all constituents have the same old local character. The claimed identity is before specialization, and the fixed F_E has its own nonzero central-value argument.
