## A008: semisimplicity and scalar commutant

Consumer: lines 694-710. Sources: Faltings 1983, section 5, Satz 3 and Satz 4, printed p. 360, and the 1984 erratum, printed p. 381. Both page images were checked. Satz 3 asserts semisimplicity of the rational Tate representation over a number field. Satz 4 identifies the integral endomorphism ring tensored with Z_l with Tate-module endomorphisms; its rational consequence is the one used here. The erratum replaces the constant-height assertion in Satz 2 by eventual stationarity and explicitly says that this suffices for the later results. It does not withdraw Satz 3 or Satz 4.

Here A is over the number field L', and every geometric endomorphism is already defined over L, hence over L'. The non-CM branch assumes End^0(A_bar)=B. Thus End_G(V_l(A))=B tensor Q_l. Scalar extension commutes with this commutant: it is the simultaneous kernel of linear commutator maps, and a finite subfamily already cuts out that kernel. The finite-dimensional algebra generated by the Galois image acts semisimply. In characteristic zero its semisimple quotient is separable, so semisimplicity persists under coefficient extension, including an algebraic closure.

After splitting B, write V=r plus r. Its Galois commutant is M_2(End_G(r)), whereas Faltings gives M_2(E). Consequently End_G(r)=E, and after further scalar extension the same argument gives the scalars. A reducible semisimple module would have a nontrivial projection in its commutant. Hence r restricted to G_L' is absolutely irreducible, and so is r on the larger group. This uses semisimplicity as well as the scalar commutant; there is no claim about residual irreducibility.

Accept conditional on A007/A061/A062 and the established non-CM endomorphism algebra. No new bound or field extension is required.

## A010: de Rham implies potentially semistable

Consumer: lines 711-717. Exact checked source: Berger's pinned author article `article02.pdf`, Theorem 0.7 on author p. 6 and Corollary 5.22 on author p. 52. The latter follows from the equivalence with quasi-unipotence of the differential equation and the monodromy result used there. The statement is that every de Rham p-adic representation is potentially semistable. The separate arXiv v3 has different numbering and is not silently substituted.

The input is a continuous finite-dimensional representation of G_Q5 over a finite extension E of Q5. Regard it first as a Q5 representation. A009 gives its de Rham property, not merely its Hodge-Tate weights. Berger gives a finite extension of Q5 on which it is semistable. The commuting E-action acts on the semistable comparison spaces, so the same conclusion holds with E coefficients. There is no Tate twist or sign conversion at this step. Pan needs existence of this extension; no bound on it is used in A012's separate prime-to-p torsion argument.

Accept conditional on A009 and the continuous finite-coefficient construction. Preserve the exact author-version locator in the audit.

## A013: semistability after adjoining torsion

Consumer: lines 780-787, with the resulting conductor use through line 810. Exact source: Silverberg-Zarhin 1995, Theorem 3.5, printed p. 406. The scanned page explicitly reads n >= 3, despite the text extractor's n > 3. It assumes n prime to the residue characteristic and that n-torsion is defined over an unramified extension of the base. Apply it with base F(A[n]); all n-torsion is then rational, so the identity extension supplies the unramified premise. Choose n=3 unless p=3, and n=5 at p=3.

For an abelian surface in characteristic zero, A[n] has rank four over F_n for these primes. Its field of definition is finite Galois of degree at most |GL_4(F_n)|. Together with [F:Q_p]<=D, this is at most DG_0. The source's Theorem 3.3, printed p. 405, gives the semistable inertia criterion: inertia acts trivially on its invariant subspace and quotient, hence unipotently on the Tate module when l differs from p. The image of wild pro-p inertia in GL_4(Z_l) is finite, since the congruence kernel is pro-l. A finite-order unipotent matrix in characteristic zero is the identity. Thus wild inertia is trivial, exactly the conclusion consumed by A012. Good reduction is not asserted after the torsion extension.

Accept conditional on A061's degree bound and A012's separately checked change of ramification numbering. The local semistability assertion itself does not depend on A012.

## A014: height of the modular Jacobian

Consumer: lines 831-840. Exact source: Javanpeykar's published ANT 8 (2014) article, Theorem 1.1.1, printed p. 90; the modular-curve discussion on pp. 92-93; section 2.1 on p. 93; section 2.3 on pp. 97-98. The theorem covers smooth projective connected curves over the algebraic closure of Q of genus at least one and gives h_Fal(X)<=13*10^6*g*deg_B(X)^5. Section 2.1 fixes the differential metric (i/2) integral omega wedge conjugate(eta). Section 2.3 identifies its curve height with the stable Faltings height of the Jacobian. These are the manuscript's original stable-height convention, with no additive normalization correction.

X_1(N) is the smooth projective connected modular curve over Q. Its map to X(1), followed by j/1728, is branched only at 0,1,infinity. Its degree is at most [SL_2(Z):Gamma_1(N)]<=N^2; passing to the effective projective action only decreases the degree. Riemann-Hurwitz for a three-point degree-d cover gives 2g-2<=d-3 and therefore g<=d. A063 gives g>=2, so the source's genus premise holds even at potentially small levels. Hence h_F(J)<=13*10^6*N^12. The premises N<=QM^2, Q>=10^12, M>=1 give h_F(J)<=Q^13*M^24 and g<=Q^3*M^4 exactly as displayed.

Accept conditional on A011's modular realization, the established level bound, and A063. The theorem does not itself extract A as an isogeny factor.

## A015: bounded isogeny in the required direction

Consumer: lines 842-857. Exact source: the pinned published Duke 163 (2014) article by Gaudron-Remond, printed p. 2058 for kappa(A) and the original stable-height convention; Theorem 1.4 on p. 2060; the definition of a couple of isogenies on p. 2059. Original page images confirm alpha(g)=2^10*g^3 and the inner exponent 64*g^2. The theorem supplies isogenies in both directions between k-defined abelian varieties which are K-isogenous, of degree at most kappa(A). It does not require principal polarizations.

Take k=K=L', source J, and target A times Z. A063 supplies the isogeny over L'. Although that construction first gives A times Z -> J, the theorem supplies J -> A times Z using the source height h_F(J). Taking logarithms of kappa(J) gives precisely the three terms in lines 846-848.

For T=QM^4, the logarithms log(14g), log d', and log max(h_F(J),log d',1) are at most 5T, 2T, and 19T. For the last term, the maximum itself is at most Q^13*M^24, including the possibly negative height. These follow from log Q<=Q, log M<=M^4/4, and Q>=10^12. The parenthesis is at most 320Q^7*M^12+40QM^4<=360Q^7*M^12. Multiplication by 1024g^3<=1024Q^9*M^12 gives 368640Q^16*M^24<=Q^17*M^24. Both the dimension powers and the exponent 24 are preserved.

Accept conditional on A014/A063 and the preceding bounds for N and [L':Q]. The separate CM use of the same published theorem at lines 591-596 belongs to A005, not A015; it is not silently accepted by this disposition.

## A062: scalar correction and sign cocycle

Consumer: lines 608-637. Exact source: Milne CFT v4.03, Chapter IV, Theorem 2.10, the Skolem-Noether theorem. It applies to homomorphisms from a simple algebra to a central simple algebra. Set both algebras equal to B over Q, with maps identity and b -> psi_sigma sigma(b) psi_sigma^-1. The latter is a Q-algebra automorphism because all the identified endomorphisms belong to B. If this automorphism is conjugation by u, replace psi_sigma by u^-1 psi_sigma, then clear denominators. This gives a B-linear actual isogeny mu_sigma from sigma(A) to A, over L because the full relevant Hom group is defined there.

The composite mu_sigma sigma(mu_tau) mu_sigma_tau^-1 is an invertible rational endomorphism of A commuting with B. Its centralizer in End^0(A)=B is Q, so the discrepancy is c in Q^times. This centralizer is not taken inside all homology endomorphisms. Let d_sigma=deg(mu_sigma)>0. Multiplicativity gives c^4=d_sigma*d_tau/d_sigma_tau since dim A=2. With a_sigma=d_sigma^-1/4 the rescaled discrepancy has fourth power one and is real, hence is +/-1. Added coefficients carry trivial Galois action, as explicitly stated in the consumer. Associativity gives the cocycle identity. The normalized choice mu_1=1 gives the normalized cocycle. Finite choices and trivial action of G_L on the Hom groups make it inflate from Gal(L/Q).

Accept conditional on A061 and the earlier geometric isomorphisms between the conjugate fibers, and on the non-CM identity End^0(A_bar)=B. No new field or degree bound is assumed.

## A063: actual Hom and complement over L'

Consumer: lines 824-831. Exact sources: Faltings 1983, section 5, Corollary 1, printed p. 361, with the 1984 erratum discussed above; Milne AV v2.00, Part I Proposition 10.1 and its proof, printed p. 42. The latter proof constructs the complement of a specified abelian subvariety using the connected kernel of its dual inclusion composed with a polarization. It works over the ground field. The note about imperfect fields is irrelevant over L'.

A011 supplies the modular realization over a common finite coefficient field; restricting to G_L' and using the two-copy identification gives the stated common constituent of V_l(A) and V_l(J). Faltings semisimplicity means common constituent here supplies a nonzero equivariant map A-to-J on Tate spaces, after coefficient extension. Invariants in finite-dimensional Hom commute with extension: a finite intersection of kernels defines the invariant space, even when the group is infinite. Therefore there was a nonzero rational l-adic invariant before extending coefficients. Faltings' Hom isomorphism then implies Hom_L'(A,J) is nonzero. It is enough that this group tensored with Q_l is nonzero; one need not claim the specific invariant matrix is an actual integral morphism.

The non-CM quaternion division endomorphism algebra makes A absolutely simple, as in the preceding branch. A nonzero morphism A -> J has finite kernel and image B0 of dimension two. Milne's complement Z to B0 in J is defined over L', and A times Z -> J is an L'-isogeny. Its dimension gives g>=2. This is the direction initially constructed; A015 supplies the opposite direction with its bound.

Accept conditional on A008/A011 and the established non-CM quaternion endomorphism algebra. No descent of Z beyond L' or integral equality of Tate lattices is claimed.
