# Fourth closure pass: central nonvanishing of the local clearing factor

Review date: 2026-11-05. Reviewer: root assistant. This is a direct source/application check of A081, not an independent second review or an acceptance of the full restricted converse.

## Exact source interfaces

The relevant control theorem is Nekovar, Selmer complexes (2006), Proposition 8.4.8.1(ii), printed pp.214-215. For a Z_p^r quotient it identifies the derived augmentation of the Iwasawa cochain complex with the original cochain complex. Its proof uses the regular sequence gamma_i-1 and the associated Koszul complexes. Corollary 8.4.8.2 gives the resulting short exact sequences for r=1, including the top-degree identification. Proposition 8.4.8.3 permits taking a Z_p^r subgroup of a quotient that also has a finite component.

The vanishing needed to use the top degree is Nekovar Lemma 8.11.3(i), printed p.256: for T in degree zero, local Iwasawa cohomology is zero outside degrees 1 and 2. This applies to a finite extension of Q_p, including p=2. Kato 2004, (12.2.3), printed p.220, separately states finite generation, torsion of local H^2, and the same degree range for the cyclotomic tower. This is the local module, not the global primitive kernel H^2_0 in Kato 13.4(2).

Local duality is Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems, second edition (2006), I Corollary 2.3, printed p.28, specialized to finite modules and passed through the compatible finite Tate modules. The field has characteristic zero, so its prime-to-characteristic hypothesis does not exclude dyadic torsion at Q_2. Weil pairing identifies V_2(E)^*(1) with V_2(E); the quadratic scalar twist has square one and preserves this self-duality. The resulting rational pairing identifies H^2(Q_v,V_h) with the Q_2-dual of H^0(Q_v,V_h), where V_h is the Tate representation of the actual quadratic twist E^h.

For finiteness of local elliptic torsion, Silverman 2009, IV Theorem 6.4(b) and Remark 6.5, printed p.132, give a torsion-free formal neighborhood whenever its depth r satisfies r>v(p)/(p-1), with normalized integral valuation. The formal group identifies this neighborhood with an open subgroup of E^h(Q_v). The latter group is compact: it is a closed subset of projective space over a locally compact field, and projective space is compact by its finite cover with integral affine coordinate charts. The quotient by an open subgroup is therefore finite. Torsion injects into this quotient and is finite. Consequently its inverse Tate module, and hence H^0(Q_v,V_h), is zero. This works at both v=2 and old odd bad primes; it requires no good reduction hypothesis.

## The actual specialization map

Let Lambda=Z_2[[Gamma]], Gamma generated by gamma, t=gamma-1, and C=R Gamma_Iw(Q_2,T_h). For the moment use the Z_2 part of the tower. The displayed control theorem gives

    C tensor^L_Lambda Z_2  = R Gamma(Q_2,T_h).

The two-term resolution [Lambda --t--> Lambda], in degrees -1,0, gives the exact sequence

    0 -> H^2(C)/t H^2(C)
      -> H^2(C tensor^L Z_2)
      -> H^3(C)[t] -> 0.

The last term is zero by the local degree range. Inverting 2 now identifies the left side with H^2(Q_2,V_h)=0. This is a derived base change followed by a top-degree conclusion. It does not assume flatness of augmentation or commute underived tensor with every cohomology group.

The full dyadic cyclotomic group has a finite order-two component. To keep its signs honest, first apply Proposition 8.4.8.3 to the Z_2 subgroup, leaving the finite component, and then invert 2. Its two idempotents are now defined. Hochschild-Serre for the order-two finite extension identifies each sign summand with cohomology of the corresponding finite quadratic twist over Q_2. Both twists are actual local elliptic curves, so the same vanishing applies to each summand. No integral division by two is claimed in this argument, which concerns height-one primes away from 2.

Put A=Lambda[1/2] on one such component and M=H^2(C)[1/2]. This is a finitely generated torsion A-module by Kato (12.2.3). The equality M/tM=0 implies M_(t)=0 by Nakayama over A_(t). Thus the order of its characteristic element at (t) is zero. After multiplying by a fixed power of 2, it has a representative in Lambda with nonzero constant term. This is the required conclusion F_2(0) nonzero. Merely knowing that the characteristic element is generically nonzero would not prove it.

## Odd old primes and uniformity

At an old odd prime q, retain W_q=H^1(I_q,V_h). Inertia has 2-cohomological dimension one for these continuous rational cochains, and the unramified quotient has cohomological dimension one. Hochschild-Serre therefore gives

    H^2(Q_q,V_h) = coker(1-Fr | W_q).

The group on the left is zero by the local duality argument above. Hence the endomorphism 1-Fr of the finite-dimensional space W_q is surjective and therefore invertible. This proves that the determinant D_q at t=0 is nonzero. Using inverse Frobenius instead changes 1-Fr by an invertible factor and does not change this nonvanishing; the manuscript's full polynomial keeps its previously fixed convention. One must not replace W_q by V_h^I(-1) at a bad prime without a separate argument.

The local quadratic squareclass groups at the finitely many old primes are finite. Thus there are finitely many possible T_h locally, and finitely many sign components. Choose characteristic elements and determinant factors for this finite list, clear their scalar denominators by a single power of 2, and multiply. Every factor has nonzero constant term, so the product F_E has F_E(0) nonzero. No factor is indexed by the number of new active primes or by the binary family dimension. The existing manuscript separately assigns D_q=1 at an active good prime and assigns its explicit Euler factor at an inactive good prime; this check does not alter that allocation.

Disposition: closed for the exact local-control and central-nonvanishing interface. The primary page images for Nekovar Proposition 8.4.8.1(ii), printed p.214, and Lemma 8.11.3, printed p.256, were inspected and confirm both the derived tensor product and the degree range. This does not prove the global class construction, the determinant comparison, or the finite-stage limit arguments elsewhere in the restricted converse.
