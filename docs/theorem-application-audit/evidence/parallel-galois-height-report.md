## A009: accept the inserted Lemma H.1

Consumer: lines 711-714; lemma and proof at 10560-10622. The lemma supplies de Rhamness and weights for r_l over Q_l, after restriction to F=L'_v identifies its double with V_l(A) tensor E. Berger and Pan consume these conclusions separately.

I independently read the pinned published Scholze article, Corollary 1.8 on pp.5-6 and Theorems 9.1 and 9.3 on pp.74-75, together with the published 2016 corrigendum. Corollary 1.8 explicitly asserts de Rhamness and identifies the associated filtered vector space with de Rham cohomology. It is stronger than its preceding Hodge-Tate assertion. The corrigendum p.2 says the results remain intact after the change to the definition of pro-etale covers; its period-sheaf corrections must also be retained. No withdrawn comparison theorem is used.

The abelian surface is smooth and proper over the finite extension F/Q_l, so its rigid analytification satisfies this corollary. Theorem 9.1 compares coherent cohomology under a proper morphism and applies to the differential sheaves. The comparison is natural for the de Rham differential, and therefore for its finite stupid-filtered complex and its hypercohomology, not merely for the separate cohomology groups. Theorem 9.3 applies to the locally constant finite sheaves Z/l^n because l^n is invertible in the characteristic-zero field F. Taking the inverse system and tensoring Q_l compares the algebraic and analytic etale representations. Naturality supplies the G_F action. The finite groups satisfy Mittag-Leffler, so no inverse-limit obstruction appears in degree one.

Milne AV v2.00, Part I Theorem 12.1(a), printed p.55, gives the canonical H^1_et = Hom(T_l A,Z_l) identification. Rationalizing and dualizing gives the covariant Tate module, without a Tate twist. Brinon-Conrad, June 24 2009, Theorem 5.2.1, pp.68-69, and Proposition 6.3.3, pp.76-77, give closure under duals and subquotients and the strict filtered duality. Thus cohomological weights 0,1 become Tate weights 0,-1 in the manuscript's stated convention. Coefficient extension with trivial Galois action is a finite direct sum on underlying Q_l-representations and preserves de Rhamness. The displayed double-copy decomposition makes the underlying Q_l-representation of r|G_F a direct summand.

For finite descent, choose finite Galois F'/K containing F. Each Fil^i D_dR,F'(W) is a finite-dimensional F'-space stable under the semilinear Gal(F'/K) action, with invariant space Fil^i D_dR,K(W). Vector-space Galois descent applied separately to each step gives F' tensor_K Fil^i D_K = Fil^i D_F'. The dimension equality proves admissibility over K. This is precisely the finite-extension proof of Brinon-Conrad Proposition 6.3.8, pp.80-81. It does not descend semistability, which would be false in general.

For the coefficientwise claim, enlarge the local field once more so that it contains the normal closure of E=C_l. Split F' tensor_Q_l E into factors indexed by embeddings of E. The geometric H^1 has graded dimensions 2,2 in each such factor. Its dual has degrees 0,-1. The two copies of r in the hypothesis are E-linearly isomorphic, hence their filtered de Rham realizations and all the embedding factors are isomorphic. Each factor therefore has graded dimensions 1,1, with no other degrees. Filtered finite descent preserves these dimensions. This is a multiplicity argument, not a claim that dimension two alone forces the weights. There is no additional quaternionic hypothesis hidden in the lemma: the required Morita conclusion is explicitly its double-copy hypothesis. In the consumer it follows from matrix units commuting with Galois and the stipulated actual Tate action over L'.

## A064: accept with a Frobenius wording clarification

Choose sigma in the decomposition group over p whose residue action is x maps to x^p on the algebraic closure of F_p. Its restriction to L fixes v and induces the p-power map on k(v). Smooth proper specialization therefore identifies its composition with bar(mu_sigma) with mu_sigma composed with sigma on the generic rational Tate module. This checks the actual map, rather than only the powers of Frobenius. The phrase 'an arithmetic Frobenius lift at v' should be clarified as specified above; the next sentence makes the intended map clear.

Milne I Proposition 10.20, printed p.50, identifies the characteristic polynomial of every actual endomorphism on all V_l with its intrinsic polynomial. Its rational trace t_sigma is independent of l. The constructed rho_l is two copies of r_l, so

    tr(r_l(sigma)) = image_in_E_l( beta(sigma)^(-1) a_sigma t_sigma / 2 ).

The scalar is independent of l because the same finite choices and embeddings are used. At five the equality with the modular trace is an equality in an injectively embedded number field, hence an equality of algebraic numbers. It transports through the chosen embedding at three.

The semisimple character input is Milne AV Part IV Lemma 3.9, printed p.143: finite-dimensional semisimple modules over a finite-dimensional algebra over a characteristic-zero field are determined by traces. For the present use let E be one common finite extension of Q_3, V=r_3 and W the corresponding modular constituent, and take R to be the E-linear span of the image of G_Q in End_E(V direct-sum W). To pass from primes to all Galois elements, both traces are continuous conjugacy-invariant functions. For every finite quotient of G_Q, Chebotarev gives a Frobenius in every conjugacy class while avoiding the fixed finite exceptional set. ## A012: accept after replacing the uninspected book citation

The file called serre-local-cft.pdf is not Local Fields. I visually read its Chapter VI title page and pp.155-161. It contains J.-P. Serre, Local Class Field Theory, in Cassels and Frohlich, Algebraic Number Theory, Chapter VI, pp.128-161, followed by Tate's Chapter VII. Relevant exact locators are Section 4.1, pp.155-157, including Theorem 1; Section 4.2, Proposition 1, p.157; and Section 4.3, pp.158-159. The first defines the Herbrand function and states upper-numbering compatibility with quotients, including the inverse-limit extension. Theorem 1 identifies the images of unit groups under reciprocity with upper ramification groups. Proposition 1 identifies abelian conductor with last upper break plus one. Section 4.3 defines the Artin conductor through the lower ramification groups. These supply the actual external interfaces needed here. The uninspected 1979 book need not remain the cited source.

Here is a full check of the bound, including the two formulas not spelled out in that chapter. Pass from an extension of degree at most a to its normal closure, whose degree is at most a!, using the faithful action on its a embeddings. Write M/Q_p for this closure, e for its ramification index, M_0 for its maximal unramified subfield, and pi for a uniformizer. The extension M/M_0 is totally ramified, generated integrally by pi, whose polynomial f is Eisenstein of degree e. The derivative terms i b_i pi^(i-1), including e pi^(e-1), have pairwise distinct valuations modulo e. Hence the term of least valuation cannot cancel, and

    d := v_M(f'(pi)) <= e-1+e v_p(e).

For each nonidentity inertia element sigma, f'(pi)=product_sigma(pi-sigma(pi)). Its valuation equals the sum of v_M(pi-sigma(pi)). By the lower-group definition on p.155, counting those integers yields

    d = sum_(i>=0) (|G_i|-1).

Thus this equality follows directly from the derivative product, without requiring a separately cited different formula. If desired, d is the usual different exponent. With the source's Herbrand derivative du/dt=|G_t|/e, the change to upper numbering gives

    d/e = 1-1/e + integral_(u>0) (1-1/|G^u|) du.

Until the last positive break b, the nontrivial group G^u has order at least two. Therefore b<=2d/e<=2(1+log_2(a!))<=4a^2. The unramified and tame cases have no positive break and satisfy the same bound. At a=1 this is immediate; for integral a>=2 use log_2(a!)<=a log_2(a)<=a^2. The manuscript's looser 4a^2 bound is valid.

For completeness the exact subgroup indexing follows from the quoted quotient theorem. In a finite Galois tower M/F/K, write G=Gal(M/K), H=Gal(M/F), Q=G/H, and use H_t=H intersect G_t from the lower definition. The quotient theorem gives |Q^u|=|G^u|/|H intersect G^u| and |Q_0|=|G_0|/|H_0|. Differentiating psi_Q(phi_G(t)) gives |H_t|/|H_0|, exactly phi_H'(t), and both functions vanish at zero. Thus phi_H=psi_Q composed with phi_G and

    H intersect G^u = H^(psi_Q(u)).

Passing to finite Galois quotients gives the absolute formula. Here F=L_v is itself Galois over Q_p because L/Q is Galois. For the auxiliary torsion field, use its normal closure as above. No unsupported non-Galois Herbrand notation is needed. Since psi_Q' >=1, psi_Q(u)>=u for u>=0.

The semistable torsion extension has degree at most DG_0 by the separately checked torsion and Raynaud inputs. Its wild inertia is trivial on V_l(A), l different from p: semistable inertia is unipotent and a continuous pro-p group has trivial image in the resulting pro-l unipotent subgroup. Thus every absolute upper group beyond U_0=4(DG_0)^2 acts trivially on the geometric Tate representation and lies in G_L.

At odd p, beta squared is tame, so beta on wild inertia has order dividing two; the pro-p condition makes it trivial. At p=2 the local norm-reciprocity identity and the stated conductor-four character give beta^4=1 on O_F^*. For e=v_F(2), logarithm and exponential converge and are inverse on U_F^(e+1) and p_F^(e+1): all nonlinear terms have strictly larger valuations there. Multiplication by four carries p_F^(e+1) onto p_F^(3e+1), hence fourth powers carry U_F^(e+1) onto U_F^(3e+1). Beta is trivial on the latter. Serre Section 4.1 Theorem 1 and Section 4.2 Proposition 1 give relative upper break at most 3e. The subgroup formula then gives triviality on absolute groups u>max(4D^2,3e), bounded by U_0. The argument consumes the existing construction of beta and its norm compatibility, without asserting a new independent closure of A068.

Finally the Artin conductor is dim(V)-dim(V^I)+Swan(V). The first term is at most two. The Swan integral is integral_(u>0) codim(V^(G^u)) du, obtained from Section 4.3's sum by the same change of variables. It is at most 2U_0. This formula uses only the finite wild image; possible infinite tame unipotent monodromy does not enter that integral. Thus 2+2U_0<=E_0, as claimed, with no factor [L':Q].
