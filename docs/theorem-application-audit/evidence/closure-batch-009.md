## 1. Idelic model and the ray-to-ring quotient

For `O_f=Z+f O_K`, use

    Pic(O_f) = A_(K,f)^* / (K^* hat(O_f)^*).

This follows from locally principal fractional ideals, not from identifying an arbitrary noninvertible O_f ideal with an ideal class. For a finite idele a, the lattice `K intersection a hat(O_f)` is locally generated by a and is an invertible fractional O_f ideal. Conversely the local generators of an invertible ideal give such an idele. Changing its generator multiplies by K^*, and changing the local generators multiplies by `hat(O_f)^*`. Away from the conductor, a uniformizer idele gives the usual positive prime ideal class.

The ray quotient at modulus f has the smaller local subgroup `1+f O_(K,v)` at conductor places, with the full unit subgroup away from them. Quotienting further by the images of rational local units gives precisely the ring-class quotient, because

    O_(f,p)^* = Z_p^* (1+f O_(K,p))

at p dividing f. To verify this equality, a unit in `Z_p+f O_(K,p)` is `a+f b` with a a p-adic unit; factor out a. The reverse inclusion is immediate. The resulting group is finite by the ray-class exact sequence, and its subgroup is open. The cited existence theorem supplies H_f, with its Artin identification. No splitting of the class-group sequence is used.

All local units at places not dividing f are killed. Thus H_f/K is unramified there. In particular the extensions used in the old local Kummer descent are unramified at 2N. This only asserts unramifiedness over K; the consumer separately assumes K splits over Q at 2N, so the completions of H_f above those places are unramified extensions of Q_p.

## 2. Conjugation and rational principal ideals

For a finite idele a of K, `a bar(a)` is the diagonal embedding of its norm idele from Q. Every finite idele b of Q can be written as a rational number times an element of `hat(Z)^*`: remove its finitely many prime valuations with a rational product of primes. Both factors are trivial in the ring-class quotient, the first in K^* and the second in `hat(O_f)^*`. Therefore

    [bar(a)] = [a]^-1 in Pic(O_f).

Conjugation preserves the defining subgroup, so H_f is normal over Q and an actual complex conjugation acts on `Gal(H_f/K)` by inversion. This proves the character identity at lines 2630-2646, with pullback on Galois action and no action on the coefficient-ring symbols t or G. It also validates the ring-class part of the uniform torsion argument at lines 2738-2750: `g^2` fixes K, and the two ring-class images in `g^2 tau g^2 tau^-1` multiply to one. The separate Tate scalar calculation remains its own algebraic input.

At a split prime q away from f, the two oriented prime ideals multiply to `(q)`. Their arithmetic Artin symbols are inverse. This gives the symmetric pair `sigma_q + sigma_q^-1` in (4.1); it is independent of an overall reversal of Artin convention. At an inert prime ell away from f, the unique prime of K is itself `(ell)`, so its Artin symbol is trivial. Since it is unramified in H_f, it splits completely there and its residue field is F_(ell^2). This is the exact premise at lines 2944-2947; it does not assert that the rational prime splits in K.

## 3. Relative ring-class group at derivative primes

For squarefree n supported on the new inert primes, the natural map `Pic(O_(fn)) -> Pic(O_f)` comes from `hat(O_(fn))^* subset hat(O_f)^*`. Its kernel is

    hat(O_f)^* / (hat(O_(fn))^* O_f^*).

Here `K^* intersection hat(O_f)^* = O_f^*`, which explains the global-unit quotient. Both O_f^* and O_K^* are `+/-1` and already lie in `hat(O_(fn))^*`. Away from n the local groups are equal. At a new inert ell the remaining quotient is

    O_(K,ell)^* / (Z_ell + ell O_(K,ell))^*
      = F_(ell^2)^* / F_ell^*,

cyclic of order ell+1. CRT at the distinct primes gives their full product, exactly (H5). This is a statement about the relative kernel, not a splitting of the full class group.

Local reciprocity identifies each such quotient with its inertia image. The rational local uniformizer ell is trivial in the global ring-class quotient: multiply its idele by the principal idele ell^-1; every remaining component is a rational local unit. Hence no extra unramified factor is introduced at that derivative prime. A generator sigma_ell may be selected with this local Artin orientation. The group-ring identity

    (sigma_ell-1) sum_(j=1)^ell j sigma_ell^j
       = (ell+1) - sum_(j=0)^ell sigma_ell^j

then follows by shifting indices and `sigma_ell^(ell+1)=1`. No division by ell+1, which is even, occurs. The subsequent Kummer descent identities require their separately stated point norm relations.

## 4. Split moving prime: the sign in the principal relation

Let r split, with an oriented pair of primes. The local unit quotient is

    (F_r^* x F_r^*) / diagonal(F_r^*) -> F_r^*,
    (u,v) |-> u/v.

There is no residual global-unit quotient because `+/-1` is diagonal. The resulting exact sequence is

    1 -> F_r^* --delta--> Pic(O_r) -> Cl(K) -> 1.

For clarity delta is the local-idelic map with the arithmetic Artin convention. Choose beta, unit at r, representing a principal relation among the selected prime ideals away from r. Let u be the idele with beta at the two places above r and 1 elsewhere. Its ratio is the reduction of `alpha=beta/bar(beta)` at the chosen orientation. Multiplying u by the principal idele beta^-1 leaves 1 at r and beta^-1 away from r. Consequently

    delta(res(alpha)) = -[(beta)]

in additive class notation. Thus the manuscript's relation

    sum_j m_j q_j + delta(res(alpha)) = 0

has the correct sign. The positive principal-ideal map in the ray exact sequence and this local-idelic delta are negatives of one another. Confusing them would reverse the required power-residue prescription.

Choose a basis of the full kernel `R=ker(Z^s -> Cl(K))`. Its principal relations, together with the kernel F_r^*, present the whole group: any relation projects to R; subtracting its basis expansion leaves a relation within F_r^*. Therefore the assignments `xi(q_j)=w_j` and `xi delta=kappa_r` extend if and only if

    kappa_r(res(alpha_a)) = -sum_j m_j^(a) w_j

for every basis relation. This checks sufficiency as well as necessity, without assuming the exact sequence splits or dividing by a class number.

For `2^n | r-1`, identify the reductions of `mu_(2^n)` with exponents in Z/2^n by the place selected in batch 8. Define kappa_r(x) by `x^((r-1)/2^n)`. For a root b with `b^(2^n)=alpha`, arithmetic Frobenius gives

    Fr_r(b)/b = b^(r-1) = alpha^((r-1)/2^n)

after reduction. Thus the multiplier prescribed on the radical in (4.4a) gives precisely the negative weighted sum in (4.4b). This verifies the sign with the source's arithmetic Frobenius convention, rather than merely declaring that conventions can be changed.

The power map is onto mu_(2^n), so kappa_r is surjective. Each local unit factor at r surjects to the residue-ratio quotient, one by u and the other by u^-1. Proposition V.5.2 therefore proves that xi is onto on inertia at either place above r. At a place above 2N the extension is unramified and its Frobenius value is zero, so xi kills the whole decomposition group. Conjugation negates its values by section 2. These are the properties used at lines 3431-3436 and 3964-4013.

## 5. The quadratic factors in the actual finite coefficients

The h used in the odd arithmetic argument is positive, squarefree, in the prepared filter containing one, and `h=1 mod 8`, as specified at lines 2195-2204. Its primes split in the chosen companion K, which is square at those primes. Hence chi_h has conductor h over Q, is unramified at 2 and the old support, and restricts to a character of the ring-class quotient of conductor divisible by h.

One can check the latter assertion locally. At q dividing h, a unit of O_f reduces to a diagonal pair `(a,a)` in the split algebra. Its norm reduces to a^2. The quadratic local character at q is therefore one on this norm. At places not dividing h it is already unramified and kills units. Global principal ideles are killed by reciprocity. Norm compatibility consequently makes the restricted chi_h trivial on the subgroup defining H_f. Choose f divisible by all the actual finite character conductors. For the individual odd-prime quadratic generators q*, the same diagonal-norm argument applies, whether q* is positive or negative. This applies to every generator of the universal rational quadratic family; their product gives the finite coefficient character on H_f. The assumptions on the actual h matter: an arbitrary quadratic character ramified at an old dyadic place is not being asserted to factor through every odd-conductor H_f.

Since the varying characters are quadratic, conjugation acts on them both trivially and by inversion. Combining them with Psi, which is anti-cyclotomic by section 2, gives `alpha^tau=alpha^-1` as used in the conjugate pairing. The coefficient ring itself is fixed in this pullback.

## 6. Norm compatibility for the genus character

At lines 8870-8879 the positive D and negative h_* are coprime odd fundamental discriminants in the stated filters. For D>1, the biquadratic extension `L=Q(sqrt(D),sqrt(h_*))` has discriminant `D h_* (D h_*)`; its quadratic subfield `F=Q(sqrt(D h_*))` has discriminant D h_*. The relative discriminant is therefore one. Thus `L/F` is unramified at the finite places and F has no real places. Its character factors through Cl(F).

Milne V.3.3 identifies that character on an ideal a prime to the support with `chi_(h_*)(N_(F/Q)(a))`. Indeed Artin at a prime above p restricts to the f-th power of rational arithmetic Frobenius, where f is its residue degree, and the ideal norm is p^f. Multiplicativity gives the formula for all prime-to-support ideals. When D=1 the restricted character is trivial. Reversal of Artin does not change this quadratic character. This checks the reciprocity map in that passage; the discriminant identity and the CM reduction theorem are separate arithmetic inputs, not consequences of V.3.3.

## 7. Orientation and Frobenius lifts in the universal correction

At a graph prime q the moving character is unramified and has value 1+t at the chosen orientation. All other graph-prime quadratic characters and the fixed character are unramified there. Only its own quadratic inertia generator g_q can change the scalar when an arithmetic Frobenius lift changes, so the possible replacement is `z_q -> g_q z_q`. In an inactive character `g_q=1`; only there is an unramified Frobenius scalar required. The active correction is one, so no unramified eigenvalue is assigned to the ramified constituent.

This also works before character evaluation. With `R=O[G]`, the two ratios `r_q=l_q/e_q^2` agree modulo `(g_q-1)`. Multiplication by `1+g_q` kills their difference because `(1+g_q)(g_q-1)=0`. The ring is two-torsion-free, so writing `r_q=1+2v_q` and cancelling two proves that `1+(1+g_q)v_q` is independent of the Frobenius lift. The existence and integrality of e_q^-1 and v_q are the explicit polynomial calculation of the consumer, not a reciprocity theorem.

Using conjugate Frobenius lifts, reversing the chosen K-orientation inverts the moving value and preserves the rational quadratic values, so z_q becomes z_q^-1. The trace a_q and determinant q are unchanged under the corresponding conjugation of the elliptic Frobenius matrix. The displayed e_q and l_q are symmetric in z_q and its inverse, hence unchanged. This verifies the orientation assertion at lines 4038-4040.
