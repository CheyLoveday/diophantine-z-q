## 1. Exact contract and order of choice

Fix an elliptic curve E/Q of conductor N and a modular parametrization phi:X_0(N)->E with phi(infinity)=0. Fix the positive odd-sign filter. Choose a negative odd fundamental discriminant h_* as in CO140–152: its central L-value is nonzero, its local squareclasses agree with the filter at 2N, its prime factors are added to the excluded support, and |h_*|>4. Write m=|h_*|. All subsequent squarefree positive D avoid that support and obey the fixed local tests. Consequently K_D=Q(sqrt(D h_*)) is imaginary, split at 2N, and has units {+1,-1}. In this odd-discriminant filter D is 1 modulo 4, and D h_* is a fundamental discriminant.

Fix an orientation at each prime power p^e exactly dividing N. Let x_D be the conductor-one CM point with that cyclic level structure. Its unaveraged genus sum is

    P_D = sum_[a in Pic(O_KD)] chi_h*(Norm a) phi(x_D^[a]).

This is the conductor-one genus point over the varying field K_D. It is not the conductor-|h| ring-class point over the fixed auxiliary field used in the determinant argument.

Choose one nonzero integer L before varying D, M, or ell. It kills the cuspidal constants in the Atkin–Lehner identities and the bounded-degree torsion used in the rational twist comparison. Fix a positive simple-zero witness D_w. The upstream Kummer construction then supplies arbitrarily large M and good primes ell, outside the entire prescribed finite support, with

    rho_T2E(Fr_ell) = C modulo 2^M,
    (h_*/ell) = -1,  (D_w/ell) = 1,

and an actual integral homomorphism

    lambda_M : E(F_(ell^2)) -> Z/2^M,
    lambda_M = b kappa_M,  b C = b.

It has Frobenius sign +1. Along the selected stages, the nonzero residue lambda_M(red_ell(LP_Dw)) has bounded exact 2-adic valuation c_w, independent of M. Additional Chebotarev prescriptions must have an actual common lift with this conjugation coset. This report does not replace that compatibility condition by independent choices.

Then there is a fixed positive integer c0, and a number field generated by fixed 2-power root characters at the primes dividing N, such that at every sufficiently advanced selected stage there is an actual classical holomorphic weight-3/2 form

    g_M = sum_(n>=0) a_M(n) q^n.

Its coefficients lie in the fixed valuation ring O_coef at 2. Its level is supported on the fixed support together with ell. The depth of this level is allowed to depend on the local Schwartz functions. Its scalar characters eta,psi have 2-power order and psi/eta=chi_(-1). For p outside the support,

    a_M(p^2 n)
      = [eta(p) a_p(E) - psi(p) (-n/p)] a_M(n)
        - p eta(p)^2 a_M(n/p^2)      modulo 2^M O_coef.       (T2.1)

For every admissible squarefree D with (D/ell)=1,

    a_M(D)
      = c0 2^omega(N) zeta_M(D)
        lambda_M(red_ell(LP_D))      modulo 2^M O_coef,       (T2.2)

where zeta_M(D) is a 2-power root of unity. The same L,c0 and coefficient field work for all D,M,ell after the fixed choices. There is no assertion that g_M is a characteristic-zero Hecke eigenform, and no assertion that lambda_M detects every D.

## 2. The actual supersingular function and fixed denominators

Let B/Q be the definite quaternion algebra ramified at ell and infinity, and R an Eichler order of level N. Let V=B^0 and Q(x)=-x^2=Nrd(x). The finite double-coset set

    X = B^times \ Bhat^times / Rhat^times

is the set of supersingular pairs on X_0(N). This includes arbitrary prime powers in N, including powers of 2. Every pair is defined over F_(ell^2). With R_z=B intersect z Rhat z^(-1), put

    P_M(z) = lambda_M(L phi_ell(z)) in Z/2^M.

This is an actual function on actual supersingular pairs. The good-prime Hecke correspondence acts through a_p(E) on the normalized quotient J_0(N)->E. The Atkin–Lehner action differs from w_(p^e) phi by its fixed cuspidal constant, which L kills. The ell-uniformizer acts as Frobenius on the supersingular set; lambda_M has sign +1. Thus the three required eigenrelations hold modulo 2^M. Choose arbitrary integer lifts of the finitely many P_M(z). No exact eigenrelation is imposed on those lifts.

Here is an explicit uniform choice for the stabilizer denominator. Every R_z^times is finite, since its elements have positive reduced norm 1 and lie in a lattice in a positive definite quaternion algebra. Left multiplication embeds it into GL_4(Z). Reduction modulo 3 is injective on a finite subgroup of GL_4(Z). Indeed a nonidentity torsion element in the kernel gives a prime-order element I+3^a A, with a>=1 and A nonzero modulo 3. Its prime-th power has a nonzero first term modulo 3^(a+1) if the prime is not 3, or modulo 3^(a+2) if it is 3, a contradiction. Hence

    c0 = |GL_4(F_3)| = (81-1)(81-3)(81-9)(81-27)
       = 24,261,120,       v2(c0)=9

is divisible by every |R_z^times|, independently of ell and z. This is a permissible fixed choice; the raw construction does not require the smallest one.

A fixed L is also legitimate. There are finitely many Atkin–Lehner cuspidal constants for fixed N; the Manin–Drinfeld theorem makes them torsion. The genus point is defined over a field of degree at most 4. For fixed E, its torsion over all such fields has bounded exponent: choose two distinct good rational primes p,q, use prime-to-p torsion injection into E(F_(p^f)) for f<=4, and the corresponding statement at q. Products of these finitely many finite group orders bound all primary parts. Multiplication by a further fixed factor 4 clears the elementary biquadratic character projections. These choices precede the Kummer stages. Enlarging L at that fixed point only adds a fixed valuation to the witness bound.

## 3. Local Schwartz functions

The finite function f is a product of the following compactly supported locally constant functions. Every nonzero value is a 2-power root of unity.

Away from 2 N h_* ell, use the characteristic function of R_p^0.

At p^e exactly dividing N, identify R_p with matrices having lower-left entry divisible by p^e. On the split fundamental unit squareclass require an integral quadratic embedding. The cyclic level quotient chooses one of the two summands of the split quadratic algebra; let r be the eigenvalue of x on that summand. Weight x by alpha_p(r), where alpha_p is a finite unit character of 2-power order satisfying

    alpha_p(-1) = w_(p^e) chi_h*(p)^e.                       (T2.3)

Such a character exists for either sign. At an odd p the 2-primary part of (Z/pZ)^times supplies the negative sign, and at 2 a character modulo 4 does so. These characters are chosen once at the fixed level primes. Conjugation by R_p^times preserves the cyclic quotient and its chosen summand. Scaling x by a unit u sends r to ur, so it multiplies the weight by alpha_p(u).

At 2, require (1+x)/2 in R_2 and x^2 a unit square. If 2 divides N, set e_x=(1+x)/2. This operator need not itself be idempotent. Its eigenvalues (1+r)/2 and (1-r)/2 differ by the odd unit r, so their spectral projectors are integral and distinguish the two summands even modulo 2. The cyclic quotient chooses one summand. For odd u,

    e_(ux) = u e_x + (1-u)/2,

which preserves the summands and sends the selected x-eigenvalue r to ur. Selection is not by the parity of the eigenvalue of e_x. This proves the root-weight transformation at every depth of 2 in N. If 2 does not divide N, there is no root character at 2.

For p dividing h_*, impose x in M_2(Z_p)^0 and v_p(Q(x))=1. Its reduction is a nonzero nilpotent. With X0=[[0,1],[0,0]], define

    epsilon_p(g X0 g^(-1)) = (det g/p).

The centralizer consists of [[a,b],[0,a]], whose determinant is a square. Thus epsilon_p is well-defined. Conjugation by u multiplies it by (det u/p), and scaling by a multiplies it by (a/p), as is seen using diag(a,1). This is the local ramified quadratic norm character required by chi_h*.

At ell, reduction from the maximal order lands in F_(ell^2). An integral trace-zero x reduces to the trace-zero line. Fix beta nonzero on that line and set f_ell(x)=(t/ell) when x reduces to t beta with t nonzero; set it to zero otherwise. Order units act trivially by conjugation on the residue field. A uniformizer induces Frobenius, sending beta to -beta. Because ell=-1 modulo 4, its multiplier is -1. Unit scaling has character (u/ell). Also beta^2 is a nonsquare, while -1 is a nonsquare, so -t^2 beta^2 is a nonzero square. Since (m/ell)=1, the extracted indices satisfy (D/ell)=1.

All extra fixed unit-squareclass conditions can be imposed by characteristic functions. Multiplication of x by a unit changes Q(x) by a square, so these conditions preserve the scaling rules. Altogether,

    f(u^(-1) x u) = chi_h*(Nrd u)^(-1) f(x),  u in Rhat^times. (T2.4)

The product of the scaling characters is even. Its value at -1 is

    [product_(p^e||N) w_(p^e) chi_h*(p)^e] chi_h*(-1) (-1) = 1.

The bracket equals the functional sign of E^(h_*), which is +1 because its central value is nonzero; h_* is negative. Let eta_B be the resulting classical character. In the inverse-idele convention of CO22–27, its value at a positive unramified prime p is the product s(p) of the finite scaling values alpha_v(p), not its inverse. This convention matters when alpha_v has order greater than 2.

## 4. Actual modularity, including coefficient extraction

Use the ordinary Weil representation attached to the nondegenerate rational quadratic space (V,Q), with self-dual Fourier measures and the standard global additive character. Its local actions are multiplication by e_p(b Q(x)) for n(b), the normalized scaling operator for m(u), and Fourier transformation for a Weyl lift. These are the operators in Weil I no.13. The one-dimensional Weil-index identities in II nos.25 and 28 give the diagonal factor relative to the ordinary theta multiplier cubed as (det Q,u)_p.

For completeness the required compact invariance follows directly from these operators. Q is integral on the support, so n(Z_p) fixes f_p. Choose a lattice L'_p under whose translations f_p is invariant. Its Fourier transform is supported in the dual lattice. Choose A_p with p^A_p Q((L'_p)^vee) integral. Then n(p^A_p Z_p) fixes the Fourier transform, so the lower unipotents n^-(p^A_p Z_p) fix f_p. This uses Q_p, including Q_2; it assumes no odd residue characteristic. At the good primes the lattice is self-dual and unramified, so no new level prime is needed.

In a presentation B=(-a,-b), Q has diagonal coefficients a,b,ab. Their product is a square. Thus the extra Hilbert symbol (det Q,u)_p is 1. After increasing the level at 2 for the standard metaplectic splitting, the factorization

    [[a,b],[c,d]] = n^-(c/a) m(a) n(b/a)

for a a unit and c divisible by p^A_p proves the scalar transformation law. The surviving finite character is eta_B. Conjugation by every z is an isometry of Q and commutes with these Weil operators. Therefore the same level and character work for every summand.

Weil III Theorem 6 gives invariance of the adelic theta sum under rational lifts. To pass from that theorem to the classical assertion, use the Gaussian at infinity. The Gaussian is the lowest-weight vector of weight 3/2. Its theta sum is the usual sum of e(Q(x) tau), which converges normally on compact subsets because Q is positive definite and the finite support cuts out a finite union of lattice cosets. At any cusp the Weil-transformed finite Schwartz function is again compactly supported; the same positive Q gives nonnegative rational exponents. Thus the scalar form is holomorphic at all cusps. The primary theorem is not being cited as if it alone stated this last holomorphy deduction.

Now define

    H_M(n) = c0 sum_(z in X) [P_M(z) chi_h*(Nrd z)/|R_z^times|]
                           sum_(x in B^0, Q(x)=n) f(z^(-1) x z).   (T2.5)

Equation (T2.4) makes the summand independent of the right representative. Rational reduced norms are positive, and the global norm character is trivial on them, so the left representative causes no ambiguity. This finite linear combination is a genuine classical form. Since c0/|R_z^times| is an integer, every coefficient lies in O_coef. Changing an integer lift of P_M(z) by 2^M changes every coefficient by 2^M O_coef.

There is a direct justification for extraction of H_M(mn), without an unproved assertion about a half-integral-weight U_m operator. At every p dividing m, the support has v_p(Q(x))=1. Hence every nonzero Fourier index of (T2.5) is divisible by m. Replace Q by the positive rational quadratic form Q'=Q/m on the same V and the same Schwartz functions. Q' is integral on their support, and the preceding upper/lower-unipotent and holomorphy argument applies again. The resulting series is exactly

    g_M(tau) = sum_n H_M(mn) e(n tau).

The determinant squareclass of Q' is m. Its diagonal Weil factor therefore adds the quadratic character (m/·). No new support prime is introduced, since all primes dividing m already belong to S. Thus the scalar character of g_M is

    psi = eta_B (m/·).

Together with eta=eta_B chi_h*, this gives psi/eta=chi_(-1). The root-character field is fixed; the moving ell contributes only quadratic values.

## 5. Full good-prime neighbor calculation

Fix p outside the support and put s=s(p)=eta_B(p). The norm-character contribution of a p-neighbor is chi_h*(p). The remaining local calculation takes place in M_2(Z_p)^0. Neighbors of a lattice are indexed by lines in F_p^2.

For a fixed integral trace-zero element of norm n with nonzero reduction, the number of invariant lines is 1+(-n/p). This includes the nonzero nilpotent case, which has one invariant line. An element with zero reduction preserves all p+1 lines.

For the nonintegral contributions, write x=y/p where y is integral and primitive with norm p^2 n. The reduction of y is a nonzero nilpotent. In a basis where it is X0, its upper-right entry is a unit and its diagonal entries are divisible by p. Since Q(y)=-a^2-bc is divisible by p^2, its lower-left entry c is divisible by p^2. Exactly the neighbor associated to the kernel line makes y/p integral.

Here is the resulting partition with all scaling factors. The primitive y contributions equal

    s^(-1) H_M(p^2 n) - H_M(n).

The subtraction removes y divisible by p. The integral contributions equal

    [1+(-n/p)] H_M(n) + p s H_M(n/p^2).

The last term is the extra p lines for zero reduction; when such an element exists, p^2 divides n. Combining the two parts gives

    s^(-1) H_M(p^2 n) + (-n/p) H_M(n) + p s H_M(n/p^2).

The stabilizer weights in (T2.5) are precisely the orbit-stabilizer weights for this correspondence, so double counting is valid even at supersingular points with extra automorphisms. Including the norm factor yields

    H_M(T_p P;n)
      = chi_h*(p) [s^(-1)H_M(p^2 n)
                    +(-n/p)H_M(n)+p s H_M(n/p^2)].

The actual function P_M has T_p P_M=a_p(E)P_M modulo 2^M. Its arbitrary integer lift need not have that identity over characteristic zero. Linearity and the integral c0 factors carry the congruence through the theta sum. Multiplying by s chi_h*(p) gives CO477–478. Substituting n=m n' gives (T2.1), with eta and psi as above. Fixed-support zero-or-retain conditions commute with this relation because p lies outside that support.

This calculation uses no spectral decomposition of g_M and no pure tensor. Such assertions are not needed to hand the actual form to the later divided-operator construction.

## 6. The CM correspondence and its exact primary hypotheses

For an admissible squarefree D, the vectors counted at norm mD are exactly the optimal embeddings of O_KD into R_z, with sqrt(Dh_*) mapped to x. At odd primes this is the usual integral quadratic generator condition. At 2, (1+x)/2 in R_2 is exactly the condition for the integral generator of the odd fundamental discriminant. An order containing O_KD is already maximal in K_D, so the embeddings are optimal.

The local count is two orientations at every p^e exactly dividing N, two at ell, and one elsewhere. At p^e|N the split quadratic algebra acts on a pair of lattices whose quotient is cyclic of order p^e. The quotient lies entirely in one idempotent summand: if both summands were nonzero, the quotient could not be cyclic. This remains valid at p=2 using the integral spectral projectors from section 3. At ell the orientations are the two residue-field embeddings. The remaining maximal-order local embeddings have a single conjugacy orbit.

The precise primary reduction result is Cornut–Jetchev, "Liftings of Reduction Maps for Quaternion Algebras", sections 3.2–3.6, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, together with sections 4.9–4.10 and Corollary 4.1. Its hypotheses in the present application are as follows.

* The base field is Q. The source quaternion algebra is M_2(Q), the changed finite set is {ell}, and the target is B. Hypothesis H.1 holds because the source is split; H.2 holds because 1+0+1 is even; H.3 holds because ell is inert in K_D.
* The level N is prime to ell but need not be squarefree. Section 4.9 constructs the actual cyclic N quotient with no restriction on powers of 2.
* Both fine conductors are 1. The ring-class field ratio in the theorem is therefore 1. Its local factor is N_ell(0,0,0)=1, from the lattice count in Lemma 2.1. Thus the correspondence has multiplicity one after the ell sign/orientation is fixed.
* Section 3.2 proves equivariance for the ideal-class/Galois action. Section 4.10 identifies the adelic map with geometric reduction and shows the supersingular pairs are defined over F_(ell^2).
* Corollary 4.1, after forgetting the ell orientation, uses conjugate pairs of embeddings. That formulation does not introduce a further factor 1/2 into the calculation below. We retain the orientation in Theorem 3.1 and count its two choices explicitly.

For a fixed full orientation tuple, choose x0 and an adelic representative z0. The class orbits are represented by a z0 for a in K_D,hat^times, modulo the class group equivalence. Since a commutes with x0,

    (a z0)^(-1) x0 (a z0) = z0^(-1) x0 z0.

Thus the local Schwartz product is constant on this ideal-class orbit. Its remaining relative norm factor is exactly chi_h*(Norm a), the genus character. The primary correspondence then identifies the class sum of the reduced values of phi with red_ell(P_D), with the same level orientation.

The embedding stabilizer is O_KD^times={+1,-1}. Its orbit under R_z^times has |R_z^times|/2 elements. Dividing by |R_z^times| in (T2.5) leaves exactly 1/2 for that conjugacy class.

Reversing the orientation at p^e|N multiplies the Schwartz weight by alpha_p(-1). The norm-weighted parametrization value is multiplied by w_(p^e) chi_h*(p)^e, the same sign by (T2.3); the cuspidal translation has already been killed by L. Their product is 1. Reversing the ell orientation multiplies the residue-line weight by -1. The norm-weighted value is also multiplied by chi_h*(ell) times its Frobenius eigenvalue, namely -1 times +1. Again their product is 1. Hence all 2^(omega(N)+1) orientation tuples contribute equally.

The phase for the initial tuple is a product of fixed root-character values and quadratic values, and is therefore a 2-power root of unity. Multiplying 2^(omega(N)+1) by the stabilizer factor 1/2 proves exactly (T2.2). No class-number average, Fourier inversion, or division by a number depending on D or ell occurs.

## 7. The fixed visible witness and precise uniformity

The modulus in this report is the ideal 2^M O_coef, with v2 normalized by v2(2)=1. It is not the M-th power of the maximal ideal of a potentially ramified coefficient ring. The field generated by the alpha_p values was fixed before M and ell, and every zeta_M(D) is a unit.

Apply (T2.2) to the one fixed D_w. At the selected Kummer stages its right side has exact valuation

    c_w + v2(c0) + omega(N).

Choose M strictly larger than this fixed number. The congruence then forces a_M(D_w) to have that same finite valuation, so g_M is nonzero and the visible coefficient persists. This statement allows passing to arbitrarily advanced members of the already compatible Kummer sequence. It does not make a simultaneous bounded-valuation assertion for all D, or for all primes ell meeting only the torsion Frobenius congruence.

The constants L,c0, the fixed character field, and this witness valuation bound do not grow with D, M, ell, the number of prime factors of D, or a later binary group dimension. The support of the level can vary by ell. Finite character depths and the dimensions of spaces of modular forms need not be uniformly bounded. No uniform bound on either is required for (T2.1)–(T2.2).
