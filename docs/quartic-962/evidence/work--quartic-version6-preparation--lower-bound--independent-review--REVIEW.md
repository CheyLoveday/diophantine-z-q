## Case audit

The topological lemma at lines 39–59 is valid. A nonempty principal open set over Q contains a real open ball avoiding all denominators. Rational points are dense there. Continuity into the closed discrete set Z makes the rational function constant on that ball, and the polynomial identity principle makes it constant identically. For a unirational variety, dominance preserves nonconstancy and permits restricting the parameter domain to the inverse image of the affine chart where the input is defined. Rational images in that chart are exactly among the inputs constrained to Z.

The degree-at-most-two argument at lines 61–89 covers all restrictions to the plane through three chosen points. A line with three zeros is contained in the hypersurface. A reducible plane restriction has at most two rational lines, forcing one to carry two distinct inputs. An irreducible quadratic plane restriction has a smooth rational point because a reduced conic has at most one geometric singular point. The possible pair of conjugate lines causes no exception: a rational point on such a geometrically reducible conic lies on both lines and is singular, so it could not supply three distinct rational points. The smooth-point conic parametrization is valid, and its input is nonconstant.

The reduction at lines 91–111 is valid. Every factor of a reducible cubic has degree at most two and its rational-zero projection is contained in the projection of the product, hence in Z. Finitely many finite projections cannot cover Z. Homogenization preserves irreducibility for a polynomial of its actual total degree. In characteristic zero the irreducible cubic is geometrically reduced. If it is not geometrically irreducible, Galois transitivity and total degree three force three distinct conjugate linear factors. Every rational zero lies on all three factors. The line through normalized input-0 and input-1 zeros therefore supplies every rational input. No geometric reducibility case is omitted.

The cone treatment at lines 113–145 is valid. The directional-derivative condition defines a rational linear kernel. In characteristic zero it is precisely translation invariance of H. Deleting this maximal kernel leaves no vertex direction. Because this is the kernel of a matrix with rational entries, scalar extension cannot create an unaccounted-for vertex direction. Thus the quotient cubic is nonconical even geometrically. Its geometric integrality follows as well: any factorization of the descended polynomial would pull back to a factorization of H.

The input argument is essential and is present. Translating any normalized rational zero along a vertex direction gives a fractional linear input. Unless its determinant is zero, its image omits at most one rational number and cannot lie in Z. Applying this first at input 0 and then at input 1 forces both input linear forms to vanish on the entire vertex subspace. They therefore descend and remain independent. A rational linear section of the quotient preserves these descended forms automatically. Hence the quotient's affine rational-point projection equals the original projection in both directions. The proof does not silently identify rational points of a quotient with arbitrary rational lifts without a section.

For quotient dimension at least two, every hypothesis of the checked Kollár theorem is met: perfect field Q, geometric integrality, noncone, and a rational point. The input is nonconstant since it assumes all integer values. The unirationality consequence then contradicts the topological lemma.

For dimension one, dehomogenizing the irreducible quotient cubic gives an irreducible polynomial in the input and one further affine coordinate. Degree zero in that coordinate has finite input projection; degree one supplies rational solutions outside a finite exceptional set. For degree at least two, Gauss's lemma gives irreducibility over Q(T). The single-polynomial integral Hilbert irreducibility statement supplies an integer specialization preserving that degree and irreducibility. Such a polynomial has no rational root. This directly contradicts coverage of every integer. No finiteness theorem for rational points on curves is being assumed.

The remaining zero-dimensional projective quotient has finitely many rational points. There is no negative-dimensional missing case relevant to the argument: the two independent descended input forms force the quotient vector space to have dimension at least two. This exhausts the possibilities.

## Scope

The draft proves that one polynomial whose rational-zero projection is exactly Z must have total degree at least four, counting the input coordinate. It is independent of all arithmetic assumptions in the proposed quartic construction. It does not establish any bound on the number of variables in a quartic. The original Brox section additionally treats arbitrary number fields and a witness-count bound; the rewritten draft intentionally proves the Q degree statement directly. This audit accepts that rewritten statement and its comparison with the corresponding degree argument in Brox.

No novelty conclusion follows from this audit. It checks correctness and use of the identified sources, not historical priority or absence of earlier formulations. No mathematical correction is required before using the audited draft. The only documentary limitation is the explicitly stated Kollár published-full-text versus checked-preprint distinction.
